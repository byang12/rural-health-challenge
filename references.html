References

Research and public resources that inform the challenge design.

Research informing the challenge

The following sources inform the program’s approach to community priorities, interdisciplinary learning, focused operational comparisons, and evaluation of AI-supported work. They include peer-reviewed studies, a federal program precedent, and a university research report. The program’s first cohort will provide evidence about the feasibility and usefulness of this specific format.

R1

Rural health challenge precedent

Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, Innovation Center. 2024 Rural Health Hackathon. Official program description.

https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/2024-rural-health-hackathon

Companion report: Re-imagining Rural Health: Themes, Concepts, and Next Steps from the CMS Innovation Center Hackathon Series.

https://www.cms.gov/files/document/reimagining-rural-health-strategy.pdf

Useful for problem selection and provider and community involvement. This is a program precedent, not an experimental evaluation of the student format.

R2

Rural community projects in education

Hinkle, H. E., Sheppard, A. B., Fess, K., Olson, H. R., and Squires, E. 2024. Key themes of community-oriented primary care projects from a longitudinal, rural interprofessional health professions curriculum (1997–2023). BMC Medical Education 24, 1215.

https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06190-8

Useful for connecting evidence, stakeholder priorities, mentoring, and feedback to communities. Its extended curriculum is more intensive than this challenge.

R3

Health professions hackathon evidence

Rooholamini, A., and Salajegheh, M. 2024. Health profession education hackathons: a scoping review of current trends and best practices. BMC Medical Education 24, 554.

https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05519-7

Useful for training and assessment design. The review highlights limitations in self-reported outcomes and long-term evidence.

R4

Organizing a small interdisciplinary health challenge

Poncette, A.-S., Rojas, P.-D., Hofferbert, J., Valera Sosa, A., Balzer, F., and Braune, K. 2020. Hackathons as stepping stones in health care innovation: Case study with systematic recommendations. Journal of Medical Internet Research 22(3), e17004.

https://doi.org/10.2196/17004

Useful for stakeholder engagement, mentoring, event preparation, and follow-up. It is a descriptive case study.

R5

Rural clinic operations and financial measures

Gale, J. A., Croll, Z., Croom, J., Munk, L., and Jonk, Y. 2022. Community characteristics and financial and operational performance of rural health clinics in the United States: A chartbook. Maine Rural Health Research Center, University of Southern Maine.

https://digitalcommons.usm.maine.edu/clinics/15/

Useful for selecting operating variables. Historical costs and payment assumptions require local updating.

R6

Focused outpatient simulation

Harder, R., Wei, K., Vaze, V., and Stahl, J. E. 2019. Simulation analysis and comparison of point of care testing and central laboratory testing. MDM Policy & Practice 4(1), 2381468319856306.

https://doi.org/10.1177/2381468319856306

Useful for comparing one change with current practice using visible assumptions. Simulation findings require validation in their intended setting.

R7

Workflow effects and financial effects

Shah, S. J., et al. 2016. Targeted reminder phone calls to patients at high risk of no-show for primary care appointment: A randomized trial. Journal of General Internal Medicine 31, 1460–1466.

https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-016-3813-0

Useful for distinguishing a measured workflow improvement from a financial benefit. This was a single-center study outside a rural setting.

R8

Evaluation of AI in outpatient work

Lukac, P. J., et al. 2025. Ambient AI scribes in clinical practice: A randomized trial. NEJM AI 2(12), AIoa2501000.

https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2501000

Useful for examining documentation time, adoption, review needs, and tool-specific effects. It does not establish rural clinic financial returns.

R9

Following projects after a challenge

Olson, K. R., et al. 2017. Health hackathons: theatre or substance? A survey assessment of outcomes from healthcare-focused hackathons in three countries. BMJ Innovations 3(1), 37–44.

https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjinnov-2016-000147

Useful for planning organizer follow-up and recording further validation. The survey design has limitations related to self-report and response bias.

R10

Efficiency and effectiveness in rural clinics

Ortiz, J., and Wan, T. 2012. Performance of rural health clinics: An examination of efficiency and Medicare beneficiary outcomes. Rural and Remote Health 12, 1925.

https://doi.org/10.22605/RRH1925

Useful for discussing efficiency alongside effectiveness and community value. Its older observational data should not be used as present-day local benchmarks.

Public information resources

  • US Census Bureau American Community Survey: https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs.html
  • CDC PLACES: https://www.cdc.gov/places/
  • HRSA Health Center Data: https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/health-centers
  • Alabama Department of Public Health rural health data: https://www.alabamapublichealth.gov/ruralhealth/health-data.html

Record the specific dataset, release year, geography, and access date when using these resources in a project. Cite the original source of an AI-supported factual claim.
