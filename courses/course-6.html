Course 6 of 6

Evidence Based Recommendations and Communication

Five short readings that turn your work from Courses 1–5 into a proposal and presentation a partner can act on.

Preparation lead: Beichen Yang

Learning goal

Present a recommendation that a healthcare or community partner can understand, assess, and use to decide on a next step.

First edition

This first edition contains the five readings. Short videos and self-check questions for each topic will be added later.

What this course covers

By Course 6, your team has a question, checked inputs, a comparison, and an AI check. This course helps you explain that work to the people who will decide what to do with it. Topics 1 and 2 show what the proposal and slides should look like. Topics 3 and 4 cover the principles to follow and the pitfalls to avoid. Topic 5 shows how to use AI to document your work as you go and turn it into drafts.

Worked example

Fictional practice case

A careful recommendation for the fictional referral case could say:

“Under the teaching assumptions, the assisted workflow could free one staff hour per week. The subscription creates an additional cash expense. We recommend a small timing and quality check before deciding whether the capacity gain justifies that expense.”

That statement makes the conditional benefit, spending implication, and next decision visible. It does not claim that staff spending has fallen or that patient outcomes have improved.

Check your work

  • Does the recommendation answer the original question?
  • Can a reader trace the main claim to a source or calculation?
  • Are financial effects, workload effects, and community benefits distinguished?
  • Is the proposed next step small enough for a partner to assess?

Before you submit, also work through the submission checklist.

Suggested reading
