Course 1 · Case materials

Case materials: source cards and workpad

Reading time: about 12 minutes

On this page

The source cards for the three referral problems, the workbook and prompt file, a guide to the four workpad sections, the minimum package for Course 2, and how the work is scored.

Use it with Topics 1 to 6. Each topic’s “Try it” box points to the part of this page you need.

Synthetic teaching material

Everything on the source cards below was written for this course, version 1. It contains no real patient records, staff interviews, measured AI outputs, or partner-approved procedures, and its identifiers do not identify real people. It is safe to paste into an approved classroom AI tool. Never put real patient information into an AI tool.

1. Workbook and prompt file

Download the workbook (Excel) Download the six prompts (text)

The workbook is a template. Definitions and code lists are filled in; the data tables, AI-run details, and review records are blank for you to complete. Start on its START_HERE sheet and work on one problem only. It opens in Excel or Google Sheets.

Where the workbook refers to “Appendix A of the main Word document,” use the source cards on this page. They are the same text.

In the one-hour core, each learner completes a small assigned part of one problem. Write down the excerpt you actually collected.

3. Problem 1 (task card P1): referral information is incomplete or scattered

Task: collect the information needed to check the supplied packets against the three-item administrative checklist. Return one row per packet and checklist item, at the first supplied version of the packet.

Basis: the study describes unclear requests for information and difficulty obtaining supporting records [1]. This checklist is a teaching adaptation, not a clinical standard.

The three-item teaching checklist
Checklist IDTeaching requirementWhat counts as supplied evidence
I_DESTDestination serviceA destination service is stated.
I_CALLCallback routeA role or channel for returning contact is stated. Do not invent a telephone number.
I_SUMAdministrative handoff summaryThe summary itself is in the supplied material. A mention on the cover sheet is not enough.

Output columns: capture_id, packet_id, checklist_item_id, extracted_value, evidence_status, source_id, source_locator, and missing_reason when needed.

Bounded extraction prompt

Use only SYN_PKT_A and SYN_PKT_B and the fixed checklist. Return each required packet/item row with its value or supporting description and exact source lines. Distinguish an attachment mention from an available attachment. Leave unsupported values empty and explain the absence. Do not add clinical requirements.

The packet snapshot and the later log are separate sources. A later request for a document does not prove the document was missing from every earlier source.

4. Problem 2 (task card P2): referral progress is difficult to reconstruct

Task: collect the written event statements from the synthetic log. Keep event dates, separately stated planned visit dates, and exact source locations. Keep unresolved referrals and conflicts.

Basis: the study reports inconsistent feedback about scheduling and concerns that referrals were lost in the communication between health systems [1]. The dates and event types below are teaching constructions, not study measurements.

SYN_LOG, version 1

Source lines in the synthetic referral log
LineText
L01Scenario cutoff: 2026-10-20, inclusive. Eligible teaching referrals: PKT-A and PKT-B. The log contains only the statements below; no times of day are supplied.
L02PKT-A | 2026-10-08 | Referral sent to specialty intake.
L03PKT-A | 2026-10-09 | Receipt acknowledged by specialty intake.
L04PKT-A | 2026-10-09 | Appointment booked for 2026-10-16.
L05PKT-A | 2026-10-16 | Visit completed.
L06PKT-B | 2026-10-10 | Referral sent to specialty intake.
L07PKT-B | 2026-10-11 | Additional administrative handoff summary requested.
L08PKT-B | 2026-10-12 | Appointment booked for 2026-10-22.
L09PKT-B | 2026-10-13 | Separate scheduling note lists a planned visit for 2026-10-23. No statement identifies which planned date applies.

Record unit: one event statement written in the log. L09 is a scheduling note, not proof of a completed visit or a corrected booking. The cutoff applies to the dates of recorded actions, not to a future date mentioned inside an eligible action.

Output columns: capture_id, referral_id, event_type, event_date, planned_visit_date, source_text, source_id, source_locator. For an absent field, leave the value empty and record a reason for that field.

Bounded extraction prompt

Extract one row per explicit statement L02-L09. Keep the action date distinct from planned_visit_date. Do not infer completed visits or resolve PKT-B’s two planned dates. Include source text and line IDs. List expected but undocumented completion/acknowledgment information separately as issues, not invented event rows.

5. Problem 3 (task card P3): follow-up responsibility is unclear

Task: use AI to draft neutral collection questions, then structure the supplied role responses without settling disagreements by guessing. Keep who reported each responsibility.

Basis: the study describes uncertainty about whether information that was sent reached its destination and was seen [1]. The role accounts below are synthetic teaching constructions.

Method: in the core, structure the written response cards. In the extended lab, two learners answer from the cards in a short role-play, and their actual answers are recorded before AI structures them. Anything a card does not specify is answered “unknown,” not made up.

Neutral collection questions for Problem 3
Question IDNeutral questionLinked field
Q_TRIGGERWhat event starts this part of your work?trigger
Q_INPUTWhat administrative information do you need at that point?required_information
Q_CONFIRMHow do you learn that the receiving team obtained the packet?confirmation_method
Q_OWNERWhen no acknowledgment arrives, who do you understand is responsible for checking receipt?reported_followup_owner

Output columns: account_id, respondent_role, workflow_step, reported_followup_owner, source_id, source_locator. Keep two accounts even though the workflow step is the same.

Bounded structuring prompt

Use only the supplied responses. Return one account per respondent and workflow step with the reported owner and source line. Preserve differences and identify a neutral clarification question. Do not generate missing answers, declare a policy, or select the “correct” owner.

A useful next clarification would be: “For this handoff, what agreed process identifies the person or role responsible for checking receipt, and how is that assignment confirmed?” A real request would go through the program organizers. This exercise does not authorize any contact with partners.

6. The four workpad sections

The topics refer to Workpads A to D. Each is a group of sheets in the workbook.

Workpad A: task, scope, and sources

Workbook sheets: CAPTURE_PLAN and SOURCE_CATALOG (Topics 1 and 2).

Record the selected problem and task, the intended user and purpose, the eligible records or exact excerpt, what one row represents, the reference period or cutoff, four to six substantive fields, the actual collection method and date, and one source limitation or access condition.

Workpad B: question-to-variable specification

Workbook sheets: QUESTION_BANK and VARIABLE_DICTIONARY (Topic 3). A worked example to adapt:

Worked example of a question-to-variable specification
DetailWorked example
Instrument / questionINST_EVENTS_v1 / Q_EVENT: “What action is stated, on what date, and is a separate planned visit date given?”
VariableV_EVENT_DATE / event_date (proposed v0.5 field; needs Course 2 alignment)
DefinitionCalendar date linked to the recorded action, not the separately stated planned visit date.
Eligible records / record unitStatements about PKT-A and PKT-B in the assigned log excerpt through 2026-10-20; one event statement.
How values are writtenCalendar date, YYYY-MM-DD. No time of day assumed. Keep the source wording.
Missing ruleEmpty date plus a reason for that field; never zero or an estimated date.

Workpad C: captures, review, and issues

Workbook sheets: your problem’s data sheet (P1_PACKET, P2_EVENTS, or P3_ACCOUNTS), REVIEW_LOG, and ISSUES (Topics 4 and 5).

Keep the unedited AI output separately; never paste a correction over the only copy. For each review decision, record the row and field, the decision, the accepted value or gap, and the reason with its source location. An example review row:

Example review decision
Row / fieldDecisionAccepted valueReason and source
Booking date (example only)CORRECT2026-10-09, not 2026-10-16L04 separates the booking action from the planned visit.

For each open issue, record an issue ID, the affected records or fields, what remains unknown or conflicting, what that means for using the data, and the proposed next source or action.

Workpad D: AI-use record and handoff

Workbook sheets: COLLECTION_EVENT and HANDOFF (Topics 4 and 6).

Record the stage and route (live run or constructed-draft review), the tool, model, and actual date, the prompt ID and version, the input source IDs and versions, where the original output and reviewed copy are saved, the most important correction or limitation, the reviewer and review date, and the package ID, version, and location.

  • Write “not reported” for live-run details the tool does not show.
  • For offline work, write “constructed teaching draft; no live run.”
  • Never invent a model version, capture date, completed reviewer check, or evidence of AI performance.

Handoff test: a receiving learner says what one row represents, traces one accepted value to its source, explains one gap or conflict, and describes what AI contributed. Record the result, the handoff status, and the next Course 2 action.

7. Minimum package for Course 2

One package, not several final reports.

Minimum contents of the Course 1 to Course 2 package
PartMinimum content
Capture planTask, purpose, eligible records or respondents, reference period, actual excerpt, record unit, intended variables, collection method, and access conditions.
Source catalog and original sourcesStable source ID, title, file or location, version, scope, evidence category, restrictions, and exact source locations. Keep the originals.
Question bank and variable dictionaryQuestion IDs and wording, linked variable IDs, definitions, eligible records, record unit, type or unit, allowed values, and a missing rule for each field.
Collection event and captured recordsLinks to the task, sources, and specification; actual collection date and route; unedited AI output; and the records for your task.
Review, AI use, and READMEReviewed copy; accepted, corrected, and rejected values with source-based reasons; unresolved issues; AI stages and run details; file list; version; next-course actions.

Keep packet, event, and account tables separate. Course 1 checks that each record matches its source; Course 2 standardizes the data and documents any changes. Sharing a referral label does not justify joining role accounts to event rows.

8. Local codes

These are project conventions for this course, not official DDI codes, clinical standards, or partner policies. The full lists are on the workbook’s CODELISTS sheet.

Missing reasons

Leave the value empty and put one of these in that field’s separate reason column. Do not use one general reason for every field in a row.

Missing-reason codes
CodeMeaning
UNKUnknown, or not supported in the supplied material
NANot applicable to this field or statement
NCNot collected in the declared scope
PENDINGWaiting for a source or permission
ERRInvalid and unresolved
SUPSuppressed or restricted

Evidence categories

Use PUBLISHED_PUBLIC for the article and TEACHING_SYNTHETIC for the source cards. A correct extraction does not turn a synthetic value into an observed clinic measurement.

9. How the work is scored

A proposed 10-point teaching rubric: up to two points for each criterion. It scores the quality of your small task, not its size or the software used.

Proposed 10-point rubric
Criterion2 points1 point / 0 points
Task and record unitFields and scope match the assigned task; the record unit is explicit.1: a small ambiguity. 0: unrelated or undefined task.
Source and instrument documentationSources, question links, definitions, and response rules support reuse.1: a minor omission. 0: essential meaning or sources missing.
AI-assisted collectionThe actual run is saved and bounded, or the offline route is honestly labeled.1: incomplete process record. 0: invented run or evidence.
Review and gapsImportant values checked; corrections and conflicts justified and visible.1: a minor check missing. 0: unsupported values accepted.
Course 2 handoffThe receiver can trace a value and identify a limitation and next action.1: a small documentation gap. 0: the package cannot be interpreted.

A live-tool run is recorded separately from offline review. A high score is not evidence that an AI product is accurate in clinical use.
