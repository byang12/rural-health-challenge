Course 5 · Lesson 1 of 5

What AI can do, today and next

Reading time: about 5 minutes

In this lesson

Describe the jobs AI already supports in clinics, and propose one new use with reasons.

You will leave with a draft of the paragraph in your proposal that says what AI does in your project and why it fits.

Key terms
AI scribe
A tool that listens to a patient visit and drafts the clinician’s note.
Prior authorization
Approval an insurer requires before it will cover certain services, such as some specialist visits.
Referral packet
The documents a clinic sends to a specialist with a referral: the referral form, insurance card, visit note, test results, and medication list.

What AI does in clinics today

When people hear “AI in health care,” many picture a machine making diagnoses. In clinics today, most AI does something more ordinary: it helps with paperwork and routine communication. In US hospitals, two of the fastest-growing uses of predictive AI in 2024 were simplifying billing and helping with scheduling (Chang et al., 2025).

Five jobs come up again and again:

  • AI scribes listen to a visit and draft the clinician’s note. The clinician reads and signs it.
  • Inbox tools draft replies to patient messages. Staff edit each draft before it goes out.
  • Prior authorization tools help prepare requests for insurers. The hard part is knowing the insurer’s current rules.
  • Referral checking lists what looks missing from a referral packet. Staff confirm each item.
  • Scheduling and reminders, one of the fastest-growing uses in hospitals.

Why these jobs came first

These jobs have three things in common. They involve a lot of routine text. They follow rules that can be written down, such as a checklist or an insurer’s policy. And a person can check the result before it reaches a patient: the clinician signs the note, and staff confirm the missing items.

A good AI job is

Routine · Rule-based · Checkable. The last point matters most: AI fits a job when someone can tell whether it got the job right.

The gains are real but usually modest

The measured gains are usually smaller than the sales pitch. In a large randomized trial, notes took about 41 seconds less to write with the better of two AI scribes, against 18 seconds less for doctors without one, and doctors still reported occasional serious errors, mostly missing information or wrong pronouns (Lukac et al., 2025). In another study, AI-drafted replies to patient messages saved no time at all, although clinicians felt less burdened (Garcia et al., 2024).

So the useful question is not only “What can AI do?” but “What should AI do here?” A small rural clinic with a few staff, a slow internet connection, or an older records system may get less from a tool than a large health system does.

Propose one new job

Now look ahead. Propose one more job that AI could do or support in rural health care, and give three reasons: the problem it solves, why AI suits it, and how you would check it.

Example idea
  • The problem: a clinic’s list of local services (transport, pharmacies, specialists) goes out of date, so calls and referrals go to the wrong place.
  • Why AI suits it: the list is long, it changes often, and updating it is routine text work.
  • How to check it: staff spot-check a few entries each week against the source. AI can produce entries that look right but aren’t, so nothing goes on the list unchecked.

Your idea does not have to be the change your team finally recommends. AI can also support your team’s own analysis.

In the practice case, the job is checking referral packets at a made-up clinic, Bluestem Family Clinic. You will try it yourself in the next lesson.

Limits to keep in mind

Most published evidence comes from large hospitals and health systems. A small rural clinic may see different results, which is why every idea needs a local check.

Use it in your project

Try it: your idea in three reasons

Write your own idea for an AI job, with three reasons: the problem it solves, why AI suits it, and how you would check it. The AI job map handout has a “Your idea” box for this. Your answer can become the paragraph in your proposal that says what AI does in your project.

Check your understanding

1. Which of these is an AI job that a team could actually check?

  1. Use AI to improve referrals.
  2. AI lists the missing checklist items in each referral packet, and staff confirm each one.
  3. AI makes the front desk more efficient.
  4. AI decides which patients need to see a specialist.
Show the answer

Answer: B. It names what goes in (the packet and the checklist), what comes out (a list of missing items), who checks (staff), and what counts as right (the checklist). That makes it checkable.

A and C are goals, not jobs: there is no way to tell whether the AI did them right. D is a clinical decision, not routine paperwork, and it is much harder to check safely. Look for a job with a clear input, a clear output, and a person who confirms it.

2. A large academic hospital found that an AI scribe saved its doctors some writing time. What should a small rural clinic conclude?

  1. The scribe will save the same time at the clinic.
  2. It might help, but the clinic needs a local check first.
  3. AI scribes do not work in small clinics.
Show the answer

Answer: B. Results from a large hospital may not carry over to a clinic with different staff, patients, internet, and records systems, but they are a good reason to test the tool locally.

Evidence from one setting is a starting point, not a promise (A). It is not proof that the tool fails elsewhere either (C). The fair conclusion is to test it under the clinic’s own conditions.

Sources
