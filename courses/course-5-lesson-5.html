Course 5 · Lesson 5 of 5

Make it work better, then test it for real

Reading time: about 5 minutes

In this lesson

Test one improvement to the AI step, and outline a small real test with rules set in advance.

You will leave with a one-page plan for a small real test, which becomes the next-step section of your proposal.

Key terms
Plan-do-study-act (PDSA) cycle
A quality-improvement method: plan a small test, run it, study the results, and decide what to do next.
Baseline
How long the job takes now, by hand, measured before any change.
Rules set in advance
Numbers agreed before the test starts that say when to continue, when to change something and test again, and when to stop.

Make the AI step work better

By now you know how the AI did on the practice packets: where it slipped, if anywhere, and what took longest to check. Some slips come from what the AI was not told or not asked to do. A few changes are worth trying:

  • Give it the referral date, and ask it to compare that date with the visit note’s date under the 90-day rule.
  • Ask it to check the page numbers on a fax.
  • Give it the insurer’s newest rules.
  • Tell it to answer “can’t confirm” instead of guessing when something is hard to read.

People can change too: if the AI often slips on names, the checker looks at names first.

Treat each change as a small test, not a fix

Change one thing, run the same packet again, and check the answer against the key. Did the slip go away? Did anything else get worse? One good answer is a start, not proof: an answer that is right once may not be right every time, so a change worth keeping is checked again before anyone relies on it. Keep a note of what you changed and what happened.

Plan a small real test

Practice packets cannot tell a clinic whether the AI step will help with its own work. Only a small real test can. Quality-improvement teams use plan-do-study-act cycles for this, starting as small as possible (Institute for Healthcare Improvement, n.d.). Guidance on pilot studies adds a second rule: decide in advance the criteria for whether and how to continue (Eldridge et al., 2016).

A one-page plan is enough:

What a one-page test plan covers
Part of the planExample
Who takes partOne staff member
How longTwo weeks
BaselineMinutes per packet by hand, measured before the change
With the AIMinutes per packet, checking included
MissesProblems the AI missed, found by a second person re-checking by hand, and the time to fix each one
Rules set in advanceIn numbers you can check: when to continue, when to change something and test again, and when to stop

Students design the test; the clinic decides

The clinic decides whether to run the test. A real test also needs a tool the clinic has approved for patient information, because practice tools and real patient data never mix. And if the test shows that the AI adds little, that is a good result: the clinic has learned something from a small, short test.

Limits to keep in mind

A small test shows whether the AI step can work in one clinic, under its own conditions. It does not prove that it works everywhere. Course 6 helps you present your plan as the next step in your proposal.

Practice activity

Try it: one change, then a plan

Pick one slip from your runs, or, if there was none, the item that took longest to check. Change one instruction to target it, re-run the same packet, and check the answer against the key (a second run is optional). Record the result on the Lesson 5 sheet. Then fill in the one-page small test plan template, starting with a by-hand baseline.

The test plan template and other practice files will be posted with the first teaching version.

Check your understanding

1. What is a small real test of the AI step for?

  1. To prove that the AI saves the clinic time.
  2. To find out whether it can work in this clinic and what to measure, with rules for continuing or stopping set before it starts.
  3. To train staff to use the new tool.
Show the answer

Answer: B. A small test checks whether the change can work here and what matters, using rules agreed in advance. It cannot prove that the AI works everywhere.

One small test cannot prove a benefit (A). Staff may learn the tool along the way (C), but that is not the purpose. The point is to learn, under rules set before the test begins.

2. You changed your instruction, and the AI now catches the old visit note in P03. What should you do before calling the slip fixed?

  1. Nothing; the slip is gone.
  2. Check that nothing else in the answer got worse, and run it again before relying on it.
  3. Add more instructions to cover every possible slip.
Show the answer

Answer: B. A change that fixes one slip can cause another, and an answer that is right once may not be right every time.

One good run is not enough (A). Piling on instructions makes it harder to tell what helped (C). Change one thing, then check the result before relying on it.
