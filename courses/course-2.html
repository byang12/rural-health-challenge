Data Curation and Integration

Preparation lead: Xiaoming Guo

Learning goal

Prepare a small, traceable dataset that can support your comparison without concealing gaps or incompatible definitions.

Keep the original information

Retain a copy of the source material and make changes in a working version. Give fields clear names, record units, and explain transformations. A short data dictionary and source notes can sit beside your analysis; they do not need to become a separate report.

Check before combining

Confirm that records describe compatible places, periods, services, and populations. A county estimate and a clinic count can provide different kinds of context, but they are not interchangeable. Check duplicates, missing values, impossible values, and differences in definitions before calculating a total or average.

Some blank fields mean unknown; others mean not applicable. Preserve that distinction. Do not replace unknown quantities with zero unless the case explicitly justifies doing so.

Distinguish input types

Label each important value as observed, estimated, or assumed. A hypothetical assisted-workflow output is an assumption for a teaching exercise. A measured task time from a documented test would be an observation. An estimate supplied by a partner should retain that description and its date.

Worked example

Fictional practice case

The fictional case includes ten packet records with assumed manual and assisted handling times. The average times are 10 and 7 minutes. Record that assisted time includes human review and correction. Combining a manual time that includes review with an assisted time that excludes it would create an unfair comparison.

The case also includes invented expected and hypothetical flagged counts of missing administrative items. Those values support a checking exercise. They do not represent results from a tested AI system or establish clinical safety.

Practice activity

Try it

Open the teaching table and identify the record key. Calculate the average handling times, check that all ten packet IDs are distinct, and verify that the input units are minutes per packet. Write a definition for each field you will use. Add a source note stating that the values are fictional teaching assumptions.

If your team’s final case uses several sources, document how you matched them and which records or variables you excluded. Explain the effect of a missing input instead of silently filling it.

Check your work

  • Can someone identify the source and meaning of every input in your main calculation?
  • Are time periods and units compatible?
  • Are unknown values still visible?
  • Can another learner repeat the transformation from the original values?

Carry these definitions and checks into your supporting artifact. Detailed cleaning scripts are optional.

Suggested reading

Gale et al. (R5), particularly its discussion of data sources and measurement limitations.
