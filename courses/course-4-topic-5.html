Course 4 · Topic 5 of 5

Test assumptions and plan continuing operation

Reading time: about 4 minutes

In this topic

Change one input to find when your recommendation would change, and name the owner, continuing funding, and review measure that keep a service running after an initial test.

You will leave with a short sensitivity table and a viability statement.

Key terms
Sensitivity test
Changing one input at a time to see how much the result moves.
Threshold
The input value at which the recommendation changes, sometimes called the break-even point.
Viability
Whether a service can keep running after the first test: who runs it, who pays, and how it is checked.
Stop rule
A result, agreed before the test starts, that means the clinic should stop or change course.

Two final questions

When would the recommendation change?

Every number in the model rests on inputs that could be wrong. Change one input, such as the assisted handling time or the tool fee, and hold every other input fixed, so the effect of that input is visible. The value at which the recommendation changes is a threshold, and it tells a partner what to watch.

Who keeps the service running?

A continuing service needs four things:

  • A named owner.
  • A source of money for each recurring cost.
  • One review measure.
  • A date to decide whether to continue, revise, or stop.

Writing the stop rule before the test starts keeps the decision honest.

Practice case: where the thresholds are

How the result is calculated

Capacity value after the fee = 20 packets × minutes saved per packet ÷ 60 × $30 − $20 fee.

The operating plan names an owner, money, and a measure

  • Owner: the practice manager (assumed).
  • Recurring cash need: $1,040 per year for the tool fee.
  • Review measure: weekly minutes per packet, including review.
  • Stop rule: stop, or renegotiate the fee, if timed packets stay above 8 minutes.
  • Possible funding: a state rural health grant, the clinic’s operating budget, partner support, or in-kind consulting from the student team during a pilot.

Limits to keep in mind

Thresholds are conditions, not forecasts

These thresholds come from invented teaching inputs. Rhee et al. (2026) found that the effects of telehealth models varied with local costs, such as transportation barriers and patients’ setup costs, so the same change can perform differently in different places. Treat each threshold as a question to test with clinic data.

A startup grant is also not continuing funding. Levi (2026) reports that the federal rural health transformation fund is not meant to pay operating expenses.

Use it in your project

Try it: write your viability statement

Make a short sensitivity table that changes one input at a time. Then complete this sentence:

“The service stays viable if [a named owner] runs it, its yearly cost of [amount] is funded from [a named source], and [a named input] stays within [its threshold].”

Use it in your proposal and presentation. Course 6 shows how to present it as your limitations and next step.

Check your understanding

1. At 20 packets a week, $30 per staff hour, and a $20 weekly fee, at what assisted time per packet, including review, does capacity value only just cover the fee?

  1. 7 minutes.
  2. 8 minutes.
  3. 9 minutes.
  4. 10 minutes.
Show the answer

Answer: B. At 8 minutes, 20 × 2 = 40 minutes are freed. That is two-thirds of an hour, worth $20, exactly the fee. A threshold from teaching inputs still has to be rechecked with local data.

A leaves $10 of value after the fee, C gives −$10, and D gives −$20. Multiply the minutes saved per packet by 20, convert to hours, value at $30, then subtract the fee.

2. A state rural health grant pays for the tool in year one. Which statement is the most complete condition for running the service in year two?

  1. The year-one grant shows that the service is sustainable.
  2. A named owner runs it, $1,040 per year is funded, and assisted time stays at or below 8 minutes.
  3. The tool drafts packets faster than staff do.
  4. Referral volume grows above 20 packets per week.
Show the answer

Answer: B. A viable service needs an owner, a source for the recurring $20 weekly cost ($1,040 per year), and an operating threshold. Year-one support does not establish continuing funding.

A treats startup money as continuing money. C ignores review time, which Course 3 showed can erase the gain. D changes volume but names no owner or funding.
