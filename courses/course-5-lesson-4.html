Course 5 · Lesson 4 of 5

What if the work is messier?

Reading time: about 5 minutes

In this lesson

Explain how messier, real-world packets change the AI’s results and the checking it needs.

You will leave with your team’s answer to “What could change your recommendation?”

Key terms
Tidy twin
The clean practice packet a messier packet was made from. Both hold the same referral, so any difference in the AI’s answer comes from how the packet arrives.
Scanned (image-only) page
A page that is a picture of text rather than typed text, as in many faxes.
What-if question
A question that changes one condition at a time, such as “What if a page is missing?”

Real packets are messier

So far the practice packets have been tidy: typed, complete pages in the right order. Real referral packets often are not. Many still arrive by fax, filled in by busy people under time pressure. A fair test of AI asks what happens when the work looks like real work.

Studies describe the same problems again and again:

  • Poor faxes. Faxed pages arrive tilted, blurred, upside down, or not at all. In one study, software could not read 17% of faxed forms, mostly for those reasons (Vlessides, 2018).
  • Handwriting. Handwritten referral forms are often incomplete or only partly legible (Chetcuti et al., 2009).
  • Missing information. Information is left out or promised for later. Specialists often report receiving no information at all from the referring doctor before the visit (Gandhi et al., 2000).
  • Changing rules. Most physicians say it is hard to tell whether a service needs prior authorization (American Medical Association, 2024).

The messier practice packets

Each messier packet copies one of these problems and is made from a tidy packet you used in Lesson 2.

The messier practice packets and their tidy twins
PacketTidy twinWhat is messier
M1P01A poor fax: the pages are scanned images, one is upside down, and one page never arrived.
M5P05An old insurance card next to the current one, and a phone number corrected by hand.
M2 (optional)P07A handwritten form.
M3 (optional)P10Information written only on a fax cover sheet.
M4 (optional)P04An insurer’s notice that changes its rules.

M3 and M4 also change what the packet holds or which rules apply, as real packets and rules do.

Compare each result with its tidy twin

Run the AI on M1 and M5 and compare each result with its tidy twin from Lesson 2. Only the way the packet arrives has changed, so any difference tells you something about the conditions. Ask what-if questions one at a time:

  • What if the page is a scan rather than typed text?
  • What if a page is missing and only the page count shows it?
  • What if a rule changes and the AI is not told?

Note what changes: new slips, new false alarms, longer checking.

Messier work usually means more checking

If the AI misses more on messy packets, someone has to find and fix those problems later. If it raises more false alarms, staff spend time chasing items that were never missing. Either way, the time saved can shrink or disappear. If the AI handles the mess well, that is worth knowing too.

Your answer to a question every team is asked

What could change your recommendation? Name the conditions that changed the AI’s results, and say which ones a real clinic is likely to face.

Limits to keep in mind

The messier packets are still made up. They copy the kinds of problems studies describe, not how often each problem happens in any real clinic. Treat your results as a reason to test further, not as proof. In Lesson 5 you will try to make the AI step work better and plan a small test on real work.

Practice activity

Try it: the Lesson 4 runs

Run the AI on M1 and M5 with exactly the same prompt as before (M2 to M4 are optional). Compare each result with its tidy twin, write down what changed on the Lesson 4 sheet, and then open the Lesson 4 answer key. Do this before you watch the video, which walks through M1 and M5.

The practice files and the name of the recommended AI tool will be posted with the first teaching version.

Check your understanding

1. The AI did well on the tidy packets. Which change would most likely shrink the time saved?

  1. Packets arrive as blurry faxes, some with pages missing.
  2. The clinic handles a few more packets each week.
  3. An experienced staff member does the checking.
Show the answer

Answer: A. Messier input tends to bring more slips and longer checking, so the saving per packet shrinks or disappears.

More packets with the same saving per packet add up to more time saved, not less (B). An experienced checker usually works faster (C). It is the messier work that eats into the saving.

2. Why test the AI on messier packets before trusting a result from tidy ones?

  1. Real packets often arrive faxed, handwritten, or incomplete, and those conditions can change how well the AI does.
  2. Messier packets make the AI look worse, which is more convincing.
  3. Tidy packets are too easy for people, so the comparison is unfair.
Show the answer

Answer: A. A fair test uses work that looks like real work. Messier packets show which conditions change the result.

The goal is not to make the AI look good or bad (B), or to handicap the people (C). It is to see how the AI behaves under the conditions a real clinic faces.
