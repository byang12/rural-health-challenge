Course 5 of 6

AI Applications and Scenario Testing

Preparation lead: Jiacheng Cao

Learning goal

Use AI for a defined task and make a fair, manageable comparison that exposes its benefits, review needs, and limitations.

Give AI a specific job

AI might help organize public information, propose a data schema, explain a calculation, identify administrative gaps in synthetic examples, or explore a scenario. Choose a task whose output your team can check. Record the task, tool, date, and the way you verified important results.

AI can be part of your analytical process even when your final recommendation is a change in scheduling, coordination, or another operating arrangement. A custom AI application is optional.

Check the whole workflow

If you test an assisted workflow, compare the same tasks and inputs under current and proposed practice. Include the time needed to prepare inputs, review outputs, correct errors, and complete the work. A fast draft is only one part of the process.

Data use with AI tools

Use the information supplied or approved for the challenge. Public, aggregate, and fictional materials are appropriate starting points. Ask the data and GIS lead before using restricted partner material with an external AI service. Keep identifiable patient information out of public course exercises and submissions.

Worked example

Fictional practice case

The teaching case includes invented missing-item counts and hypothetical assisted flags. Calculate how often the counts agree, then inspect the disagreements. Agreement occurs in 7 of the 10 fictional records. This is agreement on a simplified administrative count, not a validated measure of clinical quality or the performance of an AI product.

In a separate timing comparison, the assumed assisted handling time includes human review. Try a less favorable assumption: if assisted handling takes 10 minutes, the modeled time benefit disappears while the subscription cost remains.

Practice activity

Try it

Choose a small check appropriate to your project. You might verify AI-extracted public facts against their original sources, compare an AI-suggested calculation with a manual calculation, or use a few synthetic examples to inspect a workflow. Add a brief account of the task, check, result, and limitation to your existing proposal.

Treat any hypothetical model output as an assumption. Label an actual test as a test and describe its inputs and limits. If AI adds little value, explain that finding and its implications for the recommendation.

Check your work

  • Does your check establish what you claim it establishes?
  • Have you included correction effort and relevant tool costs?
  • Can another learner inspect the same sources or examples?
  • Is further testing needed before a partner could use the result?
Suggested reading

Lukac et al. (R8) and Harder et al. (R6).
