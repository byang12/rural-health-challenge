Course 5 of 6

AI Applications and Scenario Testing

Five short lessons on using AI with good judgment: try one AI job yourself, check every answer, and plan a small real test.

Preparation lead: Jiacheng Cao

Learning goal

Use AI for a defined task and make a fair, manageable comparison that exposes its benefits, review needs, and limitations.

First edition

This first edition contains the five lesson readings, each with two self-check questions. The videos, the practice files, and the name of the recommended AI tool will be added with the first teaching version.

What this course covers

Course 5 teaches you to use AI with good judgment. You learn what AI already does in clinics and where it could help next, try one AI job yourself on realistic practice paperwork, and check whether the result would hold up in a real clinic. That gives your team an honest account of AI’s role, how you checked it, and its limits, which your proposal needs and judges assess under “Judgment about AI.”

No coding is needed. Plan on about 45 minutes for the readings, videos, and quizzes, plus about an hour for the practice activities.

The practice case: Bluestem Family Clinic

The lessons use a made-up clinic, Bluestem Family Clinic, and one AI job: checking referral packets for missing items before they go to a specialist. You will check ten tidy practice packets (P01 to P10) against the clinic’s checklist, then five messier versions (M1 to M5) that copy problems real packets have, such as poor faxes and handwriting. A scoring sheet records the AI’s answers, its errors, and the minutes the whole job takes.

Data use with AI tools

Use only the practice packets with the recommended AI tool. Never put real patient information into an AI tool, even with names removed. Public, aggregate, and fictional materials are appropriate starting points. Ask the data and GIS lead before using restricted partner material with an external AI service.

How the course feeds your project

  • Proposal: what AI does in your project, how you checked it, its limits, and the next validation step.
  • Supporting analysis: your record of errors and timing.
  • Presentation: your answers on AI’s role and on what could change your recommendation.

AI can be part of your analytical process even when your final recommendation is a change in scheduling, coordination, or another operating arrangement. A custom AI application is optional. If AI adds little value, explain that finding and what it means for the recommendation.

Check your work

  • Can you name the AI job, what goes in, what comes out, and who checks it?
  • Did you check every answer item by item and sort errors into misses and false alarms?
  • Did you time the whole job, including checking and fixing?
  • Have you said how messier, real-world work could change the result?
  • Is your next step a small real test with rules set in advance?
Suggested reading

Lukac et al. (R8) and Harder et al. (R6).
