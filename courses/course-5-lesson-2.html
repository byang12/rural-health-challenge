Course 5 · Lesson 2 of 5

Try the AI on real-looking work

Reading time: about 6 minutes

In this lesson

Run an AI tool on the practice packets, check every answer against the answer key, and time the whole job.

You will leave with a scoring sheet that becomes your team’s account of how you checked your AI.

Key terms
Miss
A real problem the AI did not flag. It costs time later, when the specialist’s office sends the packet back. A guessed value also counts as a miss.
False alarm
A problem the AI flagged that is not there. It costs time now, when staff go looking for something that was never missing. Flagging an item that is readable counts as a false alarm.
Can’t confirm
An honest answer when something is hard to read. It is better than a guess.
Answer key
The correct result for each packet. Open it only after all your runs.

Give the AI a real clinic job

Reading about AI only goes so far. In this lesson you give an AI tool a real clinic job, checking referral packets, and see for yourself what it does well and where it struggles. The packets come from a made-up clinic, Bluestem Family Clinic, but they look like the real thing: a referral form, an insurance card, a prior authorization approval, a visit note, test results, and a medication list.

Two rules first
  • Use only the recommended AI tool. You will be told which one.
  • Use only the practice packets. Never put real patient information into an AI tool. Removing names is not enough to make real data safe: federal privacy rules list 18 kinds of identifiers, including dates linked to a person, phone numbers, and medical record numbers (U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, 2012).

What staff check, and what the AI gets

Staff check each packet against the clinic’s eight-item checklist and a short table of which insurance plans need prior authorization for which specialties. The AI needs exactly the same information. If you leave out the plan rules, you are testing your instructions, not the AI.

The steps

  1. Start by hand. Check one packet, P02, against the checklist yourself and time it. That gives you something to compare the AI with.
  2. Give the AI the same rules. Paste the prompt from the practice instructions, with the checklist and the plan rules. It asks the AI to go item by item and say whether each item is present, missing, or can’t be confirmed, and where it looked.
  3. Run the AI and time the whole job. For packets P01, P03, P05, and P08, start timing when you open the packet and stop when you have checked the AI’s answer against the packet and noted what needs fixing. Also note when the AI’s first answer appears: that is the part the AI speeds up. The time that matters is the total time with a person in the loop.
  4. Run one packet twice. Run P08 again in a new chat with the same prompt. AI tools do not always give the same answer to the same question, and an answer that is right once is not necessarily right every time (Shyr et al., 2025).
  5. Then open the answer key. When all your runs are done, compare item by item, not just the totals, and sort each error into a miss or a false alarm.

Why compare item by item

Two answers can both say “one item missing” while one of them names the wrong item. And the two kinds of error cost different kinds of staff time. A miss costs time later, when the specialist’s office sends the packet back. A false alarm costs time now, when staff go looking for something that was never missing.

Limits to keep in mind

Ten made-up packets teach you how to check an AI tool. They cannot tell you how well a real tool will do on a real clinic’s packets. Lesson 4 takes you one step closer.

Practice activity

Try it: the Lesson 2 runs

Check P02 by hand, then run P01, P03, P05, and P08 with the AI, running P08 twice. Record each answer, error, and time on the scoring sheet, then open the Lesson 2 answer key. Do this before you watch the video, which walks through the same packets so you can compare your results with ours.

The practice files and the name of the recommended AI tool will be posted with the first teaching version.

Check your understanding

1. The AI’s count of missing items matches the answer key on three of the four packets. What should you do next?

  1. Report that the AI is 75% accurate.
  2. Compare the actual items in every packet, and sort the errors into misses and false alarms.
  3. Switch to a bigger AI model.
Show the answer

Answer: B. Matching counts can hide errors that cancel out: one miss plus one false alarm in the same packet still gives the right count. And the two kinds of error take different kinds of staff time: a miss means rework later, a false alarm means chasing now.

Counts alone can make the AI look better than it is (A). Changing tools before you know what went wrong teaches you nothing (C). Check the items, not just the totals.

2. Which of these can go into the AI tool for this exercise?

  1. The practice packets.
  2. A partner clinic’s referral list with the patient names removed.
Show the answer

Answer: A. The practice packets are made up, so they are safe to use. A partner’s patient list stays out, even with the names removed: it is still real patient information.

Removing names is not enough: dates, phone numbers, and record numbers can still identify someone. Real patient information stays out of AI tools in this course. Other restricted partner material needs approval from the program’s data and GIS lead before it goes into an outside AI tool.
