Course 4 · Topic 3 of 5

Identify setup and recurring costs

Reading time: about 5 minutes

In this topic

Sort costs into setup and recurring, write each as quantity times unit cost for a stated period, and keep unknown costs visible.

You will leave with the cost rows of your practice table.

Key terms
Setup cost
A cost that happens once, before or at the start of a change, such as training or configuring a tool.
Recurring cost
A cost that repeats for as long as the change runs, such as a subscription, staff review, or maintenance.
Resource value
The value of staff time or other resources used, even when no new money is paid.
Cash cost
Money that actually leaves the clinic, such as a new subscription fee.

How to list costs

Costs differ by when they occur

Setup costs occur once: training, configuring a tool, or redesigning a form. Recurring costs repeat for as long as the change runs: subscriptions, staff review, coordination, maintenance, and transport. Separate the two and state the time period of every cost. Mixing them hides the amount the clinic must keep paying after launch.

Each cost row has a quantity, a unit cost, a period, and a status

Write each cost as quantity times unit cost for a stated period, such as “3 hours × $30 per hour, once.” Keep the period consistent: weekly in the working table, yearly in the summary. Label each row as observed, estimated, assumed, or unknown, so a reader can see which numbers come from evidence. A guess is acceptable when it is labeled as a guess.

For real inputs, the course’s suggested reading by Gale et al. (2022) is a chartbook built from Medicare cost reports and other national data on rural health clinics. Its historical costs and payment figures need local updating before use.

Practice case: one setup cost and one new payment

Payroll does not change, so the setup uses staff time without a new payment. Training time is still a real resource cost, even though no invoice is paid for it.

Some recurring costs are not yet known

Keep unknown costs in the table as rows marked “unknown,” each with the evidence that would price it. Entering $0 would claim that the cost does not exist, and deleting the row would hide it. Ask who could supply each missing price, and by when, so the gap has an owner.

Limits to keep in mind

Resource value and cash belong in different columns

Staff time has a resource value even when no one receives a new payment, so a cost table needs one column for resource value and another for cash.

Grants also fit setup costs more easily than recurring costs. Levi (2026) reports that the federal Rural Health Transformation Program lets states use at most 15% of its funds for direct payments to providers and that, in the words of the CMS administrator, its purpose is not to pay operating expenses.

Some costs appear only later. Kamalahmadi et al. (2026) found that some effects of telehealth, including added workload and lost revenue, became visible only after adoption. That is another reason to keep unknown rows in view.

Use it in your project

Try it: build your cost rows

Fill the setup and recurring-cost columns of your practice table with quantities, unit costs, periods, and status labels. When you update a number, keep the same row and note the change, so the history of each estimate stays visible. Topic 4 compares these costs with the benefit, and Topic 5 asks who pays the recurring rows.

Check your understanding

1. Which list splits the costs correctly into setup and recurring?

  1. Setup: training and tool configuration. Recurring: subscription, review, and maintenance.
  2. Setup: subscription. Recurring: training.
  3. Setup: review time. Recurring: form redesign.
  4. All costs are setup when a grant pays for them.
Show the answer

Answer: A. Training and configuration happen once; the fee, review, and maintenance repeat every week. Because federal rural health funds are not meant to pay operating expenses, recurring costs need their own rows and their own funding source.

B and C reverse the timing. D mixes who pays with when a cost occurs. Sort by timing first; the funding source comes later, in Topic 5.

2. The team cannot yet estimate tool maintenance. What should the cost table show?

  1. $0, because the cost is unknown.
  2. Nothing; leave the row out.
  3. A maintenance row marked unknown, with the evidence needed to price it.
  4. Double the subscription to be safe.
Show the answer

Answer: C. State which costs you can estimate and which remain unknown. Costs that are not yet priced should stay in view, because some only become visible after a change starts.

A and B hide the cost, and D invents a number. Keep the row, label it unknown, and name who could supply a price.
