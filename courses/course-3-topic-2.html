Course 3 · Topic 2 of 5

Establish the baseline

Reading time: about 5 minutes

In this topic (Stage 2 of 4)

Describe what happens now, and calculate the current workload with a clear boundary, unit, and time period.

You will leave with baseline totals and rates that anyone can trace back to the data rows.

Key terms
Baseline
How the work is done now. Every option is compared against it, using the same referrals and the same time period.
Staff minutes
Active time people spend working. If two people each work five minutes, that is ten staff minutes.
Elapsed time
Calendar time that passes, including waiting. A referral can wait days for an appointment while nobody is working on it.
Boundary
What the calculation counts and what it leaves out.
Denominator
The number you divide by when you work out a rate. In this case it is all 20 referrals.

Why the baseline comes first

The baseline is your starting point. You need to know what staff do, how much of that work the data record, and which outcomes you can actually see. Without it, any claim that an option is “faster” has nothing to be faster than.

What happens now: the referral workflow

This six-step map rebuilds documented referral work for the classroom. Steps 1 to 5 draw on activities reported in S2, and step 6 also uses the clinic example in S3. A real clinic’s steps and roles must be confirmed locally before anyone acts on them.

Baseline referral workflow in six steps
StepWhat happensWho does it
1The clinician orders a consultation and gives the clinical reason.Referring clinician
2Staff review the request and discuss the referral with the patient.Coordinator and patient
3Staff find an available specialist and check location, insurance, and any required authorization.Coordinator
4Staff assemble the order and supporting records, check the packet, and send it the way the specialist accepts.Coordinator
5The specialist office and the patient or coordinator arrange the appointment.Specialist office and patient
6Staff track unresolved items and the specialist’s information coming back to the clinic record. Clinical staff review and act on the results.Coordinator and clinician

In the case, the clinic keeps a general referral log but has no standard packet checklist. That detail is a teaching assumption, not a description of St. Luke’s or any other named clinic.

Where the data fit

Each of the 20 records has three time fields. They do not overlap, so you can add them.

PrepMin
Preparing the referral, from first handling until the packet is sent, including the usual manual check that it is complete (roughly steps 2 to 4).
CorrectMin
Extra work after an incomplete packet comes back and has to be fixed.
FollowMin
Later calls, status checks, and logging the specialist’s report (roughly steps 5 and 6).

What the calculation leaves out

Clinical assessment, the clinician’s review of results, patient travel time, specialist treatment, and anything after day 30 sit outside the staff-time boundary. They still matter to the decision. No option may drop necessary clinical review or patient support just to look faster.

Work one row by hand

Before asking AI for totals, calculate one record yourself. Here is referral R03:

Staff minutes for referral R03
PrepMinCorrectMinFollowMinStaff minutes
128212 + 8 + 2 = 22

Once you can trace R03 cell by cell, ask AI to show the same calculation. If its numbers for R03 differ from yours, stop and find out why before going further.

The baseline formulas

  • Total recorded coordination time = sum of PrepMin + sum of CorrectMin + sum of FollowMin.
  • Mean preparation time = total PrepMin ÷ number of referrals.
  • Returned-packet rate = number of rows with CorrectMin above zero ÷ all referrals.
  • Documented attendance rate = rows with Visit30 = Y ÷ all referrals. Report the unknowns separately.

Read the status codes carefully

  • U is not N. A Visit30 of U means nobody knows whether the patient attended. It is not a confirmed missed visit.
  • Report30 = N means no specialist report was logged by day 30. It does not prove that care never happened.
  • A booking is not a visit. Booked30 = Y can mean an appointment booked for a date after day 30.
  • A logged report is not a closed loop. The data do not show whether a clinician reviewed the report or acted on it.

Ask AI to help, with limits

Prompt to adapt: calculate the baseline

Check the dataset for missing values and inconsistent statuses. Calculate the baseline totals and rates requested in the worksheet. For each, show fields, inputs, units, denominator, and formula. Keep unknown outcomes separate. Do not treat missing data as zero or turn staff minutes into cash savings.

Practice activity

Try it: worksheet Stage 2

Using the 20 records in Topic 5, complete the baseline table: preparation, correction, and follow-up totals; total staff minutes; returned packets; and attendance and report counts. Check one calculation independently, and name one activity that falls outside the time boundary.

Check your understanding

1. A coordinator spends 5 minutes and a nurse spends 5 minutes on one referral. The referral then waits 3 days for an appointment. How many staff minutes does it use?

  1. 10 minutes
  2. 3 days
  3. 5 minutes
Show the answer

Answer: A. Staff minutes count active work by every person: 5 + 5 = 10.

The 3 days are elapsed time (B). Nobody worked on the referral the whole time it waited. Counting only one person (C) leaves out work.

2. Some referrals have Visit30 = U. How should they appear in the documented attendance rate?

  1. Count them as missed visits.
  2. Leave them out of the data entirely.
  3. Keep them in the denominator of all referrals, do not count them as attended, and report the unknowns separately.
Show the answer

Answer: C. The documented rate divides confirmed attendance by all referrals, and you report the unknowns next to it so a reader sees what is still uncertain.

A U is not a confirmed missed visit (A). Dropping the unknowns (B) would make the rate look more certain than the data allow.
