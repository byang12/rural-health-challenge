Course 3 · Topic 1 of 5

Understand the problem

Reading time: about 6 minutes

In this topic (Stage 1 of 4)

State the decision the clinic must make, name the people affected, and sort what you know into evidence, assumptions, and unknowns.

You will leave with a one-sentence problem statement and a short evidence list in which every item carries a label.

Key terms
Referral
A request from a primary-care clinician for a patient to see a specialist, such as a cardiologist. Staff prepare a packet of records, send it, help arrange the visit, and track the specialist’s report back to the clinic.
Operating pressure
The strain on the clinic’s everyday work that makes a change worth considering, such as staff time spent on referral paperwork.
Decision
The choice the clinic can actually make. In this case: how to prepare referral packets.
Synthetic data
Invented records made for practice. They look realistic but describe no real patient or clinic.
Evidence label
A short tag that says where a claim comes from, so a reader knows how far to trust it.

Start with the decision, not the tool

Before you ask AI for solutions, tell it what decision you need to make, whose work is involved, and what information you actually have. A slow referral could come from packet preparation, scheduling, transportation, or reports that never come back. Each of these needs a different fix, so find where the problem is before you choose a change.

In this course’s case, Teaching Clinic A is a fictional rural clinic. Its coordinator handles referrals alongside other office work, and the manager wants to cut avoidable paperwork without removing patient support or necessary checks. Your team compares three ways of preparing referral packets. The full case is in Topic 5.

The real problem behind the case

The clinic and its numbers are invented, but the problem is real. Four published sources show that referral coordination is a genuine burden for rural clinics.

What each source shows and what it does not show
SourceWhat it showsWhat it does not show
S1. A rural clinic study (Biggerstaff and Short, 2017)A review of 3,814 referrals from ten primary-care providers over six months in 2015 found that nearly half of referred patients did not make the specialist appointment.A detailed workflow, or data you can use in class.
S2. Referral work across clinics (Ezeonwu, 2018)Staff at 37 Washington State community health clinics described their referral steps and their mix of phone, fax, and electronic communication.A standard rural process: only three of the clinics were rural.
S3. A named clinic practice (Improving Primary Care Team Guide)St. Luke’s Eastern Oregon Medical Associates uses a dedicated referral manager, faxes packets with a cover sheet asking for confirmation and an appointment, and tracks the specialist’s information back into the clinic record.When the practice was last updated.
S4. A rural improvement program (HRSA sourcebook, 2022)Teche Action Board improved referrals across eight rural Louisiana clinics by changing staff roles, training, electronic records, tracking, and audits, with continued support for referral positions.Whether AI works. It is a program report, not a trial.

What this evidence lets you say

These sources show that referral coordination is a real operating problem, and they support the workflow steps you will use in Topic 2 and the need for someone to own the process. They do not show that AI saves a particular number of minutes, that it improves referral completion, or that any Alabama clinic works this way.

Real problem, invented numbers

The referral problem and the workflow sources are real. Every record, time, cost, limit, and option result in the case is invented for teaching. None of it was measured at the named organizations. Before treating the case as an analysis of a real partner, have that partner’s referral coordinator confirm the steps, roles, systems, failure points, and local timing.

Label every item

As you collect facts for the case, give each one a label. Labels stop an invented number from being mistaken for a measured one, and they show a reader exactly what still needs checking.

The five evidence labels used in Course 3
LabelMeaningExample from the case
Published evidenceA claim supported by a sourceA real clinic asks specialists to confirm receipt of referrals. [S3]
Synthetic case recordAn invented observation for practiceReferral R03 has 12 preparation minutes.
Scenario assumptionA value to test, not a measured benefitAI-assisted preparation could take 6 minutes.
Calculated resultA value worked out with a formula you showTotal time is the sum of three time fields.
UnknownNot established by the materialsWhether either option improves attendance.

Write a problem statement that leaves the solution open

A good problem statement names the work being compared and the people affected. It does not pick the answer in advance.

Weak

“AI is needed because referrals are slow.”

It chooses a tool before finding the problem, and “slow” could mean paperwork, scheduling, travel, or missing reports.

Stronger frame

“The clinic needs to decide [the choice it can actually make] because [the documented pressure]. The people affected are [who does the work and who feels the result].”

Think beyond the coordinator. Who else is touched by a referral, from the person who orders it to the office that receives it?

Ask AI to help, with limits

Paste the case materials first, then give AI a prompt like this one. Check every label it assigns against the source or the data row it cites.

Prompt to adapt: frame the decision

Use only the supplied materials. State the operating pressure, affected people, and decision in one sentence. Make an evidence list labeled published evidence, synthetic case record, scenario assumption, calculated result, or unknown. Give source IDs or dataset fields. Do not invent missing information or choose a solution yet.

Practice activity

Try it: worksheet Stage 1

Complete the problem statement frame above for Teaching Clinic A. Then record one published source and what it supports, one synthetic input, and one unknown. The worksheet is in Topic 5.

Check your understanding

1. Which is the better starting point for the analysis?

  1. “AI is needed because referrals are slow.”
  2. “The clinic needs to find where referral work is lost before it chooses a change.”
Show the answer

Answer: B. It points at the work to be examined and leaves the solution open.

A picks the tool first. If the real delay is scheduling or transportation, a faster way to prepare packets would not help, and the analysis would never find that out.

2. “Referral R07 has 8 preparation minutes.” Which label fits?

  1. Published evidence
  2. Synthetic case record
  3. Scenario assumption
  4. Calculated result
Show the answer

Answer: B. R07 is one of the 20 invented referral records, so its 8 minutes is an invented observation for practice.

It is not published evidence (A), because no source measured it. A scenario assumption (C) is a value you choose to test, such as how long AI-assisted preparation might take. A calculated result (D) comes from a formula, such as a total across all 20 rows.
