Course 1 of 6

Rural Health Data Collection

Preparation lead: Fabricio Gutierrez Juarez

Learning goal

Turn a focused rural healthcare question into a purposeful plan for obtaining the information needed to assess it.

Start with the decision

A useful collection plan begins with a question a provider can act on. “Understand every healthcare need in the county” is too broad for this challenge. A manageable question might ask whether a specific change in appointment hours or referral preparation could make better use of existing resources.

Write down who would use the recommendation, what decision they face, and what information could change that decision. Collect data because they inform that choice.

Identify three kinds of information

Community information describes the population and access context. Public sources may help you understand population, transport, insurance, or health conditions.

Operating information describes the service or workflow, such as available staff time, appointment slots, handling time, costs, or referral volume.

Local interpretation explains what the numbers miss, including opening hours, service eligibility, transport availability, and staff experience.

Use the provided case materials first. Add a public source or request partner clarification when an important gap remains. Organizers coordinate any direct partner contact.

Make a small information plan

For each required input, record the variable, unit, time period, geographic area, intended use, source, and uncertainty. “County population in a particular year” and “clinic visits each week” are different quantities. A disease-prevalence estimate does not directly establish the number of appointments a clinic will receive.

Select one public source that fits your question. Census ACS data can help with community context; CDC PLACES offers local population health estimates; HRSA resources help identify health-center services. Links and cautions are provided on the Resources page.

Worked example

Fictional practice case

In the fictional referral case, the decision is whether a small administrative change deserves further testing. Useful inputs include weekly packet volume, current staff time per packet, assisted time including review, subscription cost, and the purpose for which freed staff time would be used. A county-wide inventory of all equipment is not needed to answer this particular question.

Practice activity

Try it

Use the shared case to write one decision question. Select four to six inputs that would help answer it. For each input, identify whether it is supplied, available from a public source, a partner estimate, or an assumption. Identify one gap that could materially change the recommendation.

Check your work

  • Does each input serve the decision you named?
  • Are units, geography, and dates clear?
  • Have you distinguished a source observation from an assumption?
  • Could a partner explain why a public statistic might not describe the clinic’s actual work?

Use the resulting notes in the evidence section of your final proposal. A separate collection report is not required.

Suggested reading

Hinkle et al. (R2) and the rural clinic chartbook by Gale et al. (R5).
