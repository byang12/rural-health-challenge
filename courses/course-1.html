Course 1 of 6

Rural Health Data Collection

Three referral problems, one AI-assisted collection workflow, and a documented handoff to Course 2.

Preparation lead: Fabricio Gutierrez Juarez

Learning goal

Use AI at each stage of a specific data-collection task (defining requirements, selecting sources, designing questions, capturing information, checking results, and preparing documentation) and produce one collection package, traceable to its sources, that Course 2 can curate and combine.

First edition

This first edition contains the six topic readings, each with a self-check question, plus the case materials, workbook, and prompt file. The short videos will be added after recording. Your instructor holds the answer key and the constructed practice draft.

What this course covers

Course 1 teaches you to use AI for part of the collection work while staying in control of it: you decide what to collect, from which sources, with which definitions, and you check what the AI returns. By the end, you can:

  1. Identify the information, sources, record unit, and collection method a specific administrative referral task needs.
  2. Give AI a bounded instruction to draft a question set or extract information, and check its important outputs against the supplied evidence.
  3. Create a versioned handoff that links questions, variables, sources, captured records, notes on AI use, and visible gaps.

You need basic spreadsheet skills. No programming, clinical training, or paid AI platform is required. The one-hour core is about 30 minutes of reading, 18 minutes of video, and 12 minutes of checks and small workpad tasks. Completing all three problems on your own is an extended lab.

One workflow, six steps

Topics 1 to 3 prepare the collection specification, Topic 4 runs a small AI-assisted capture, Topic 5 checks it, and Topic 6 packages it for Course 2. The demonstrations use all three problems; you complete a small assigned part of one problem in the same workbook, building it up topic by topic.

The prompts in each topic are starting instructions you can copy. They do not guarantee how any tool will behave.

The case: three referral problems

The course imagines a referral handoff from a rural clinic to an urban health system, based on a published study in which 44 people described slow referrals, difficult contacts between providers, and computer record systems that did not connect (Sutherland et al., 2020).

1. Information is incomplete

What administrative information is in two synthetic referral packets?

2. Progress is hard to follow

Which events and dates in a synthetic log show how each referral moved forward?

3. Follow-up is unclear

What do two staff roles each say about who checks that a referral arrived?

Real problem, synthetic records

The study is real and supplies the reason to look at referral coordination. The packets, log, role cards, and checklist were written for this course. They are not study data or a partner’s records, and they contain no real patient information.

What “DDI-inspired” means

The Data Documentation Initiative (DDI) is an international standard for describing data. This course borrows a few of its ideas in a simple spreadsheet: every captured record links back to its task, its question or extraction instruction, its variable definition, and its source. You do not need to produce any DDI technical files, and the course’s local codes are not official DDI codes.

How the course feeds your project

  • Supporting artifact: one collection package: capture plan, source catalog, question-to-variable links, captured and reviewed records, and notes on AI use.
  • Proposal: the evidence section, including where each input came from and what is still missing.
  • Course 2: receives the package to standardize, check, and combine.

The package is not an extra final report. Your required outputs remain the proposal, one supporting analysis or demonstration, and the presentation. If your team’s own question also needs community context, public data sources and cautions are listed on the Resources page.

Check your work: the handoff test

Ask another learner to:

  • say what one row of your table represents;
  • trace one accepted value to its source line;
  • explain one missing value or conflict;
  • describe what AI contributed, and how you checked it.
