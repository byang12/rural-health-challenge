Course 4 of 6

Clinic Service and Business Model Development

Preparation lead: Don Ma

Learning goal

Explain whether your proposed improvement could operate with realistic staffing, resources, and funding.

Describe the change in operational terms

State who would do the work, what would change, what resources would be needed, and who would oversee it. Use a simple process diagram or short description. Include a partner only when that partner has a meaningful role in the proposed change.

For this challenge, business reasoning means understanding the resources, costs, funding, and value associated with your specific idea. Apply that reasoning at the scale of the question you chose.

Identify the relevant costs

Separate setup effort from recurring costs. Setup might include training, configuring a tool, or changing a form. Recurring costs might include subscriptions, staff review, coordination, maintenance, or transport. Specify the time period and state which costs you can estimate and which remain unknown.

Explain what “benefit” means in your model: cash spending avoided, additional collections, staff capacity, more dependable access, or another relevant measure. If continuing funding is needed, make that need visible.

Worked example

Fictional practice case

The fictional referral case assumes one hour of staff capacity could be freed each week. At an illustrative staff-cost value of $30 per hour, that capacity has an estimated value of $30. A hypothetical tool subscription costs $20 per week. The comparison therefore leaves $10 of modeled weekly capacity value after the subscription cost, before setup effort.

The case assumes three staff hours of setup, valued at $90. Payroll remains unchanged in this example: the subscription creates a cash expense, while the freed time creates a potential capacity benefit. A partner would need to decide whether that extra capacity is useful enough to justify the spending.

Practice activity

Try it

Prepare a small table showing the proposed change, responsible person or role, setup needs, recurring cost, expected benefit, and the evidence still needed. Change either the assisted handling time or subscription cost and explain whether your recommendation changes.

Include only the financial detail needed for your question. A full clinic business plan, detailed billing model, and 12-month cash-flow forecast are optional extensions.

Check your work

  • Are staff time and cash spending distinguished?
  • Are review, training, and maintenance included where relevant?
  • Are the units and time periods consistent?
  • Have you explained who would operate the change after an initial test?
Suggested reading

Gale et al. (R5) and Shah et al. (R7).
