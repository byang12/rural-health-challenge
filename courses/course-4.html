Course 4 of 6

Clinic Service and Business Model Development

Five short readings on whether your proposed change could run with realistic staff, costs, and funding.

Preparation lead: Don Ma

Learning goal

Explain whether your proposed improvement could operate with realistic staffing, resources, and funding.

First edition

This first edition contains the five readings, each with two self-check questions. Short videos for each topic will be added later.

What this course covers

Course 3 helped you choose one change for the clinic. Course 4 asks whether that change could keep running: who does the work, what it costs once and every week, what the clinic gets back, and what must hold for the service to continue. A rural partner can act on a recommendation only when it names the work, the costs, and who pays after startup funding ends.

For this challenge, business reasoning means understanding the resources, costs, funding, and value associated with your specific idea. Apply that reasoning at the scale of the question you chose. No business or finance background is needed.

Worked example

Fictional practice case

The fictional referral case assumes one hour of staff capacity could be freed each week. At an illustrative staff-cost value of $30 per hour, that capacity has an estimated value of $30. A hypothetical tool subscription costs $20 per week. The comparison therefore leaves $10 of modeled weekly capacity value after the subscription cost, before setup effort.

The case assumes three staff hours of setup, valued at $90. Payroll remains unchanged in this example: the subscription creates a cash expense, while the freed time creates a potential capacity benefit. A partner would need to decide whether that extra capacity is useful enough to justify the spending.

Practice activity

Try it

Prepare a small table showing the proposed change, responsible person or role, setup needs, recurring cost, expected benefit, and the evidence still needed. Change either the assisted handling time or subscription cost and explain whether your recommendation changes.

Each topic fills part of this table. Here is the table completed for the practice case:

Practice table completed for the practice case
ColumnPractice case example
Proposed change (Topic 1)Assisted referral packets with nurse review, a local-first resource directory, and a transport check.
Responsible role (Topics 1–2)Coordinator prepares; nurse reviews; practice manager oversees (assumed roles).
Setup needs (Topic 3)Training and configuration: 3 staff hours, $90 of staff time.
Recurring cost (Topic 3)Tool fee of $20 per week ($1,040 per year); review is inside the 7 minutes; maintenance unknown.
Expected benefit (Topic 4)1 staff hour per week ($30 capacity value) for follow-up and transport calls; cash spending rises $20 per week.
Evidence still needed (Topic 5)Timed packets including review; a maintenance price; whether follow-up raises referral completion.
One changed input (Topic 5)At 8 assisted minutes, the capacity value only covers the fee, so the change needs a lower fee or more volume.

Include only the financial detail needed for your question. A full clinic business plan, detailed billing model, and 12-month cash-flow forecast are optional extensions.

Check your work

  • Are staff time and cash spending distinguished?
  • Are review, training, and maintenance included where relevant?
  • Are the units and time periods consistent?
  • Have you explained who would operate the change after an initial test?
