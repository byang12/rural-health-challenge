Course 4 · Topic 1 of 5

Describe the service change

Reading time: about 4 minutes

In this topic

Describe your proposed change in operational terms: who does the work, what changes, what resources it needs, who oversees it, and which partners have a real role.

You will leave with the first paragraph of your operating model.

Key terms
Service model
A plain description of how a change works on an ordinary day.
Operational terms
Words about people, actions, and resources (“a nurse reviews each packet”), rather than about technology or goals (“use AI”).
Overseer
The person who checks that the change is working, such as a practice manager.

What a service model is

It says how a change works on an ordinary day

When a team proposes a change to a rural clinic, the clinic’s first question is practical: what will people do differently on Monday morning? A service model answers that question by naming four things:

  • Who does the work.
  • What changes in the work.
  • Which resources are needed.
  • Who oversees the result.

A short description or a simple process diagram is enough.

A product name is not a service model

Teams often begin with a tool, such as an AI drafting assistant, and stop there. But a tool is only one resource inside a service. Rhee et al. (2026) studied telehealth in community health centers and found that it is not one intervention but several service designs, and the two designs they compared were linked to different results for access and for follow-up. The same lesson applies to your project: the design around the tool decides what the clinic gains, so the design is what you must describe.

Practice case: an assisted referral service

Fictional practice case

The clinic prepares 20 referral packets each week. Under the proposed change:

  • Who and what: a referral coordinator prepares each packet with a software tool, and a nurse reviews the packet before it is sent.
  • Oversight: the practice manager checks a weekly referral log.
  • Resources: the tool, staff time, and a local resource database from the student team, which acts as a supplier while the clinic remains the customer.

These roles are teaching assumptions. Clinic staff would need to confirm them.

A partner belongs in the model only when it performs a task

Include a partner only when it has a meaningful role in the change. In the case, county services and transport providers receive referrals, and city specialists (for example, at DCH or at UAB with Children’s of Alabama) receive patients. Each performs a task in the workflow, so each earns one line in the model.

Bravo et al. (2026) found that residents of socioeconomically vulnerable communities are more sensitive to travel distance, and that useful partner sites fill gaps in existing networks. That is why the service checks options near the patient’s county before sending a patient to the city.

Limits to keep in mind

A clear service model does not show that the change will work. It shows what would have to happen for it to work, which makes the next questions possible: how long the work takes (Topic 2), what it costs (Topic 3), and who pays (Topic 5).

Partner roles in this course are examples from team notes, not agreements. Do not present them as commitments.

Use it in your project

Try it: write your service statement

Write three or four sentences that name the role, the action, the resource, the overseer, and each partner’s task. Put them at the start of the operating-model section of your proposal and in the “change” and “role” columns of your practice table. Topic 2 then adds the steps and times.

Check your understanding

1. Which statement describes the referral change in operational terms?

  1. The clinic will use AI for referrals.
  2. A coordinator prepares each packet with a tool, a nurse reviews it, and the practice manager checks a weekly log.
  3. Referral software will make the clinic sustainable.
  4. The clinic will partner with a city hospital.
Show the answer

Answer: B. Only B names who does the work, what changes, the resource used, and who oversees it.

A and C name a technology or an outcome, not an activity. D names a partner without its task. Rewrite the change with a role, an action, a resource, and an overseer.

2. When should a partner appear in the service model?

  1. Always; list the largest nearby hospital.
  2. When it performs a task in the change, such as receiving referrals or providing transport.
  3. Only when it pays for the service.
  4. Never; partners make the model too complex.
Show the answer

Answer: B. Include a partner only when it has a meaningful role in the change. A nearby partner earns its place by doing a task, such as filling a service gap close to the patient’s home.

A adds a partner for its size, C confuses funding with operating roles, and D drops partners who do real work.
