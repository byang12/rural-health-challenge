The referral case workbook

Reading time: about 10 minutes

In this topic

Everything your pair needs for the case: the one-page case reading, the 20 referral records, the inputs for each option, the worksheet, and starter prompts.

You will submit the completed worksheet, a four-to-six-sentence recommendation, and a record of one AI output you checked, corrected, or rejected.

Data use with AI tools

Every record on this page is invented and contains no real patient information, so it is safe to paste into a classroom AI tool. Never put real patient information into an AI tool.

1. The case: Teaching Clinic A

Read this first. It takes about three minutes.

The decision

Teaching Clinic A is a fictional rural clinic. A coordinator handles referrals while also doing other office work. The manager wants to reduce avoidable administrative effort without removing patient support or necessary checks. Your team must compare current practice, a standard checklist, and AI-assisted packet preparation.

The case addresses a real problem. A published rural-clinic study found that many referrals did not reach the specialist appointment, and a named Oregon clinic shows a referral manager coordinating information and confirmations (S1, S3). These sources support the problem and the workflow, but the numbers you will analyze are invented for learning.

The records

You receive 20 invented referral records. To keep the comparison simple, all of them start on day 0 and are observed through day 30. The shared start is a classroom simplification, and 30 days is an observation window, not a clinical deadline. All referrals are routine; decisions about clinical urgency stay with the clinician.

Current practice uses manual packet preparation and a general referral log. Staff send records, handle returned packets, check progress, and log specialist reports. Your team can change packet preparation. You cannot assume more specialist appointments, better transportation, or higher attendance.

Use the low, middle, and high review-time scenarios. Decide which option deserves a small pilot, and explain what must be checked before wider use. A recommendation for the checklist can be just as successful as one involving AI.

Working conditions

  • Use the same 20 referrals and 30-day cycle for every comparison. Keep the listed correction and follow-up work in every option.
  • The manager can consider up to $120 of additional cash per cycle and 300 staff minutes of one-time setup. These are teaching limits, not real clinic budgets.
  • A trained person reviews every AI draft before use. Existing clinical responsibilities continue. No option may add unexamined burdens for patients or receiving clinics.
  • Use only these invented records with classroom AI.

Field guide

All time fields are active staff minutes during the same 30-day window.

PrepMin
Initial administrative preparation, including the normal manual checks.
CorrectMin
Extra work after sending, to correct a packet that came back incomplete. Each row above zero is one affected packet.
FollowMin
Later calls, status checks, and logging the specialist’s report.
Booked30
Y = an appointment is documented as booked by day 30, even if the visit date is later. N = confirmed not booked. U = unknown.
Visit30
Y = documented attendance by day 30. N = confirmed no attendance by day 30. U = attendance status unknown.
Report30
Y = specialist report logged by day 30. N = no report logged by then. A logged report does not prove that a clinician reviewed it or that the referral loop is closed.

These records do not show why a packet was incomplete. Do not infer a cause from a status alone.

3. The three options: scenario inputs

Every performance value, cost, limit, and setup time below is an invented assumption, and the named sources do not validate any of them. Use r = 3 minutes as the middle scenario, then test r = 1 and r = 5.

The 6 minutes of AI preparation does not include r. Review means checking the draft against the source records and correcting draft errors before sending. CorrectMin is later work on packets returned after sending, so it is a separate activity.

What each option requires

  • Checklist: a standard list of required fields and records, with a coordinator assigned to keep it current. The 8 minutes includes using and checking the list. The extra 10 minutes is process maintenance for the whole cycle.
  • AI assistance: an approved tool drafts a cover sheet from supplied information and flags missing fields, and a trained coordinator checks each draft. The 20 minutes covers routine template and tool support for the cycle. Existing clinical review stays outside the time model and unchanged.

Cash values are extra spending compared with current practice, not total clinic costs or vendor prices. Setup minutes are staff capacity, not automatically extra payroll. One-time cash is zero only because this classroom scenario supplies the equipment and tool setup; a real site would have to check that.

Decision rules

  1. First check each option against the cash and setup limits.
  2. Then compare staff capacity and explain the cash tradeoff. The case puts no dollar value on a minute saved, so the option with the lowest time is not automatically best.
  3. Address quality, access, and who will own the process over time.

The case gives no evidence that either option changes returned packets, attendance, or report receipt. Keep those benefits unknown. Holding correction and follow-up constant is a starting assumption that a real pilot must test.

5. Starter prompts

Paste the case, the field guide, the records, and the scenario inputs into the AI chat before asking for calculations. The prompts are starting points: your pair stays responsible for checking every result. Work in pairs, with one person running the AI and the other checking its inputs and calculations; switch roles after the baseline.

Prompt 1: frame the decision

Use only the supplied materials. State the operating pressure, affected people, and decision in one sentence. Make an evidence list labeled published evidence, synthetic case record, scenario assumption, calculated result, or unknown. Give source IDs or dataset fields. Do not invent missing information or choose a solution yet.

Prompt 2: calculate the baseline

Check the 20 records for missing values and inconsistent statuses. Sum PrepMin, CorrectMin, and FollowMin separately and together. Calculate mean preparation time, returned-packet rate, Visit30 Y N U counts, documented attendance rate, and report-receipt rate. Show formulas and denominators. Keep unknown attendance separate from confirmed nonattendance. Explain what these data cannot tell us.

Prompt 3: compare the options

Using the scenario sheet, compare current practice, the checklist, and AI assistance at r = 3. Keep the same 20 referrals and 30-day period. Show preparation, review, corrections, follow-up, maintenance, recurring total, setup time, first-cycle total, and incremental cash. Check the stated resource limits. Do not claim lower payroll or better attendance from a staff-time calculation.

Prompt 4: test the decision

Recalculate AI assistance at r = 1, 3, and 5. Hold all other inputs fixed. Find the review time that gives equal recurring staff time to the checklist and to current practice. Explain the time and cash tradeoff. Propose a small pilot with an owner and monitoring measures. Identify one assumption and one missing outcome that could change the recommendation.

Final challenge prompt

Review our draft recommendation. Identify any claim not supported by our sources or calculations, any work omitted from the model, and any burden transferred to patients or another organization. Suggest a correction. Do not create new data.

6. Check before you submit

  • Trace at least one input to its row or source, and reproduce one result by hand.
  • Confirm that every comparison uses the same 20 referrals and 30-day window, and includes maintenance and human review.
  • Describe each scenario result as conditional, and do not present unknown outcomes as demonstrated benefits.
If AI access fails

Use the same worksheet and formulas with a calculator or spreadsheet. Your instructor can share a sample response after you have attempted the calculation.

7. How the worksheet is scored

The worksheet is scored out of eight: up to two points for each criterion.

Scoring criteria out of eight points
CriterionTwo pointsOne point or zero
EvidenceInputs and claims correctly labeled and traceableSome labels missing, or unsupported claims

SensitivityCorrect scenarios and an explanation of how the comparison changedResults shown with limited interpretation, or incorrect logic
DecisionConditional, feasible within the stated limits, with owner and evidence gap identifiedConditions, ownership, or evidence gaps missing
