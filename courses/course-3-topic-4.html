Course 3 · Topic 4 of 5

Test assumptions and defend the recommendation

Reading time: about 5 minutes

In this topic (Stage 4 of 4)

Change one uncertain input, see whether your conclusion holds, and say when it should change.

You will leave with a three-scenario table and a four-to-six-sentence recommendation that states what it depends on.

Key terms
Sensitivity analysis
Changing an uncertain input and checking how the result changes. It does not prove that the changed value will happen.
Threshold
The input value at which two options come out equal. On one side of it one option is better; on the other side, the other one is.
Conditional recommendation
A recommendation that says what it depends on and what would change it.
Pilot
A small, time-limited trial run to collect real evidence before a wider change.

What a sensitivity test is for

You are testing whether your recommendation depends on a number nobody has measured yet. You are not claiming that the low or high value will actually happen.

In this case, the most uncertain input is r, the minutes a person spends reviewing each AI draft. Nobody has timed it at Teaching Clinic A, and it could easily be longer than hoped.

A one-packet demonstration

For one AI-assisted packet, 6 minutes of preparation plus 1 minute of review gives 7 minutes. If review takes 5 minutes, the total becomes 11.

One AI-assisted packet at two review times

1 minute6 + 17
5 minutes6 + 511

The same uncertain input can make one option look better or worse. Comparing only 7 minutes with the baseline’s average 10 minutes of preparation would leave work out. The full exercise also counts corrections, follow-up, and maintenance.

The main test

  1. Recalculate the AI option at three review times: 1, 3, and 5 minutes per referral. Keep every other input exactly as it was in Stage 3.
  2. Compare each result with current practice and the checklist.
  3. Find the thresholds: the review time at which AI takes the same recurring staff time as the checklist, and the review time at which it matches current practice.
Hint

Write the AI option’s recurring time as one formula with r in it: 20 referrals × (6 + r) minutes, plus the correction, follow-up, and maintenance minutes. Set that formula equal to another option’s total and solve for r.

The values 1, 3, and 5 are teaching scenarios. They are not statistical confidence intervals or measured performance. Equal time is not the same as equal value: an option can tie on minutes and still cost more cash.

What a defensible recommendation includes

  • The option you prefer.
  • The evidence you checked.
  • The setup and continuing resources it needs, and who owns it.
  • A condition that would change your decision.
  • An outcome that still needs to be measured.

A recommendation can be a small pilot rather than full adoption. Recommending the checklist can be just as strong an answer as recommending AI, and so can collecting more evidence first, if you name the uncertainty that stops you from deciding. State the tradeoff openly rather than hiding it in a single score.

Overreaches

“Our option saves time, so it will reduce payroll and increase referral completion.”

Matches the evidence

“The model suggests that [minutes] of recurring staff capacity could be released each cycle. Payroll savings and higher completion have not been established.”

Ask AI to test, then to challenge

Prompt to adapt: test the decision

Recalculate the AI-assisted option at review times of 1, 3, and 5 minutes per referral. Keep other inputs fixed. Compare it with current practice and the checklist. Find the review-time thresholds for equal staff time. Explain the cash tradeoff and startup effort. Draft a conditional recommendation and identify evidence that could overturn it.

Then turn AI into a critic of your own draft:

Prompt to adapt: challenge the draft

Review our draft recommendation. Identify any claim not supported by our sources or calculations, any work omitted from the model, and any burden transferred to patients or another organization. Suggest a correction. Do not create new data.

Ask yourselves three questions as well: Which sentence goes beyond our evidence? Whose work or access could get worse? What should we measure before expanding the change?

Practice activity

Try it: worksheet Stage 4

Record the AI option’s recurring minutes at review times of 1, 3, and 5 minutes, and the two thresholds. Then write four to six sentences covering your chosen pilot, the evidence, the resources and owner, the condition that changes the comparison, and one outcome to measure next. Finish with your AI use record. The worksheet is in Topic 5.

Check your understanding

1. What does testing review times of 1, 3, and 5 minutes tell you?

  1. How long review will actually take at the clinic.
  2. Whether your recommendation changes if the uncertain review time changes.
  3. A 95% confidence interval for the AI option.
Show the answer

Answer: B. A sensitivity test shows how much your conclusion depends on an input you have not measured.

Only a real pilot can tell you the actual review time (A). The three values are teaching scenarios, not a statistical interval (C).

2. Which recommendation is most defensible?

  1. “AI will save the clinic money and improve referral completion.”
  2. “Pilot [option] for one cycle, owned by [role]. If measured review time is above [threshold], the comparison changes. We will measure preparation, review, corrections, attendance status, and report receipt before expanding.”
  3. “Choose whichever option has the lowest minutes.”
Show the answer

Answer: B. It names an option, an owner, the condition that would change the decision, and the outcomes still to be measured.

A claims savings and better access that the model never showed. C ignores cash, setup effort, quality, access, and ownership: the lowest-minute option is not automatically the best choice.
