Course 3 · Topic 3 of 5

Compare feasible alternatives

Reading time: about 5 minutes

In this topic (Stage 3 of 4)

Compare current practice with two possible changes, counting all the work and resources each one needs.

You will leave with a comparison table for the three options at the middle review time of 3 minutes per referral.

Key terms
Cycle
One group of 20 referrals observed over 30 days. Every option is compared on the same cycle.
Recurring staff time
The staff minutes an option takes every cycle.
One-time setup
Staff minutes spent once to get started, such as building a template and training staff.
First-cycle staff time
Recurring time plus setup: the total for the first cycle.
Incremental cash
New money the clinic would spend compared with current practice, such as a tool fee.
Continuing owner
The named person who keeps the process running after it starts.

An option is a proposal, not a result

Any time an option promises is an assumption until someone measures it. A fair comparison also counts the work needed to check, correct, and maintain the new process, not just the part that gets faster.

The three options

Current practice

Manual packet preparation and a general referral log, as recorded in the 20 baseline records.

Checklist

A standard list of the fields and records every packet needs, kept up to date by a named owner.

AI assistance

AI drafts a cover sheet from information already supplied and flags missing fields. A trained person checks and corrects every draft before it is sent.

In all three options, clinical responsibilities stay the same. The AI cannot invent clinical content, decide how urgent a referral is, or send a referral by itself. The time and cost inputs for each option are in the scenario table in Topic 5.

Count all the work, not just the fast part

The calculation structure

Recurring staff time = preparation + required human review + corrections after sending + follow-up + maintenance.

First-cycle staff time = recurring staff time + one-time setup and training.

For AI assistance, the review is a separate line. The 6 minutes of AI-assisted preparation does not include the person’s review, so add r, the review minutes per referral. Review means checking the draft against the source records and fixing errors before sending. Corrections are later work on packets that come back after sending, so the two are counted separately.

Current practice and the checklist show zero for separate review. That does not mean nobody checks: their ordinary checking is already inside their preparation time.

Keep cash in its own column

Staff minutes and dollars are different things, so keep them apart. Salaries do not shrink when staff have spare minutes, so freed time is capacity, not savings. Setup minutes are staff time, not automatically extra payroll. The case also gives no figures for revenue, reimbursement, or financial return, so do not estimate them.

Check the clinic’s limits too. The manager can consider up to $120 of extra cash per cycle and 300 staff minutes of one-time setup. These limits are part of the fictional case, not a real clinic budget.

Hold the rest constant, and say so

Both new options keep correction and follow-up work at the baseline level. That is a comparison assumption: it keeps the comparison simple, but it is not evidence that this work would stay the same. The case gives no measured improvement in attendance, report return, or errors, so keep those benefits listed as unknown.

Unsupported claim

“Faster preparation will improve attendance.”

What it would take

Evidence about scheduling, the barriers patients face (such as transportation), and actual follow-up outcomes. None of these is in the case data.

Ask AI to help, with limits

Prompt to adapt: compare the options

Compare the three supplied options for the same 20 referrals and 30-day cycle. Use only the scenario sheet. Show recurring staff time, one-time setup time, first-cycle time, and incremental cash separately. Include required review and maintenance. State which claimed quality or access benefits cannot be estimated. Identify the continuing owner.

Check that the AI’s table includes the review line and keeps setup separate from recurring time. These are the two places where comparisons most often go wrong.

Practice activity

Try it: worksheet Stage 3

Using the scenario inputs with a review time of 3 minutes, fill in recurring minutes, setup minutes, first-cycle minutes, and extra cash for each option. Then check each option against the clinic’s cash and setup limits, and name who would keep it running.

Check your understanding

1. A teammate writes, “Faster preparation will improve attendance.” What is the best response?

  1. Agree: less paperwork means patients get seen sooner.
  2. Flag it as unsupported: attendance also depends on scheduling, patient barriers, and follow-up, and the case has no evidence on any of them.
  3. Put a number on it, such as a 10% attendance gain, so the claim can be tested.
Show the answer

Answer: B. Preparation time is one step in a longer chain. Whether a patient attends also depends on appointment availability, transportation, and follow-up, and the case measures none of these.

Agreeing (A) assumes the link without evidence. Making up a number (C) turns an unknown into a fake result. Keep attendance listed as an outcome still to be measured.

2. Which belongs in the AI option’s recurring staff time?

  1. Only the 6 minutes of AI-assisted preparation per referral.
  2. Preparation, human review, corrections, follow-up, and maintenance.
  3. Preparation plus the one-time setup and training.
Show the answer

Answer: B. Recurring time counts all the work that repeats every cycle, including the person who checks each draft.

Counting only preparation (A) leaves out the review and makes AI look faster than it is. Setup (C) happens once, so it belongs in first-cycle time, not recurring time.
