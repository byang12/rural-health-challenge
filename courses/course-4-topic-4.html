Course 4 · Topic 4 of 5

Define the benefit: capacity versus cash

Reading time: about 5 minutes

In this topic

Explain what “benefit” means in your model, and keep staff-capacity value, cash spending, and access in separate, labeled rows.

You will leave with the benefit rows of your practice table.

Key terms
Capacity value
The dollar value of freed staff time at an illustrative wage. It shows what the time is worth, not money saved.
Cash spending
Money the clinic actually pays out, such as wages or a tool fee.
Measured, modeled, assumed
Labels for a claim: measured in a documented test, calculated from inputs (modeled), or taken as a starting value (assumed).

What “benefit” can mean

Benefit has several meanings

A benefit can mean cash spending avoided, additional payments collected, staff capacity, or more dependable access for patients. These have different units: dollars of cash, dollars of resource value, hours, or completed visits. An improvement in one does not establish an improvement in another, so each needs its own row and its own evidence.

Every claim needs a label

Label a measured result as measured, a model result as modeled, and an assumption as assumed. A benefit row that adds several outcomes into one number cannot carry a single label, which is one more reason to keep rows separate.

Outcomes of one service can move in different directions. Rhee et al. (2026) found that one telehealth model was linked to more access to mental health care, while another was not linked to more access on average but was linked to better follow-through on screening and follow-up.

A workflow gain is not a financial gain

Shah et al. (2016), a suggested reading for this course, ran a trial in which targeted reminder calls lowered the no-show rate from 29.2% to 22.8%, while payments to the clinic did not differ significantly between the groups. The study was not rural and did not test referrals, so it shows the distinction rather than predicting your clinic’s result. It also teaches a reading habit: rely on the reported results, not only on a summary sentence.

The freed hour has value only if it gets useful work

At the illustrative $30 per hour, the freed hour is worth $30 a week, but only if the clinic gives it useful work, such as follow-up and transport calls for referred patients. Name that task in the operating plan, so the hour has a clear use and a person who checks it.

Cash moves in the other direction

The clinic keeps the same staff and wages, so payroll does not change. The tool fee is a new payment, so the clinic’s cash spending rises by $20 a week. A partner then decides whether one extra hour of capacity is worth $20 a week of new spending.

Limits to keep in mind

Capacity value is not revenue

The $10 figure is a resource value on invented inputs. It is not cash saved, revenue gained, or a return on investment. Kamalahmadi et al. (2026) found that telehealth expanded access to physicians while shifting their work in ways that contributed to revenue losses for the health system. A gain in access or capacity can sit beside a financial loss, and a careful proposal reports both.

Show the $20 cash increase in the same table as the $30 capacity value, so no reader sees one number without the other.

Use it in your project

Try it: one row per benefit

Fill the “expected benefit” column of your practice table with one row for each kind of benefit, each with a unit and a label. Use the same rows in your presentation, so judges can see which claim rests on which evidence. Topic 5 then tests how these numbers change when an input changes.

Check your understanding

1. The assisted workflow frees one staff hour per week, valued at $30. The tool costs $20 per week, and payroll does not change. What happens to weekly cash spending?

  1. It falls by $10.
  2. It falls by $30.
  3. It rises by $20.
  4. It does not change.
Show the answer

Answer: C. Payroll stays the same, so the freed hour is capacity, not a cash saving. The only new payment is the $20 fee. The $10 is capacity value after the fee, not cash.

A and B treat capacity value as cash, and D misses the new fee. Ask which payments change this week: wages do not, the fee does.

2. A team reports one benefit number that adds freed staff time, cash, and better follow-up together. What is the problem?

  1. None; one number is easier to present.
  2. These benefits can move separately, so each needs its own row and evidence.
  3. Follow-up should be left out because it is hard to price.
  4. Cash should be converted into minutes first.
Show the answer

Answer: B. Capacity, cash, and access are different outcomes measured in different units, and the outcomes of one service can move in different directions.

A hides which benefit is real, C drops an outcome, and D still merges the units. Give each benefit its own row, unit, and label.
