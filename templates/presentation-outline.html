Presentation outline

Six slides for an eight-minute presentation by three speakers. Each headline states a point, and each number matches your proposal.

How to use this outline
  • Use 5–7 slides for eight minutes, followed by questions. Six slides is a guide: merge slides 1 and 2, or add a second diagram, if your project needs it.
  • Download the PowerPoint version, or rebuild these slides in any slide tool.
  • Write each headline as a sentence that states the slide’s point. Use one main visual and no more than three short bullets per slide.
  • Use the same numbers, units, and definitions as your proposal and supporting artifact.
  • Name the file TeamName_Presentation.

Timing plan

SpeakerSlidesTimeWords to plan
Speaker 11–2About 2½ minutesAbout 275–325
Speaker 23–5About 3 minutesAbout 330–390
Speaker 36, then opens questionsAbout 2½ minutesAbout 275–325

Word counts assume about 110–130 spoken words per minute. Rehearse with a timer and aim to finish in about 7½ minutes.

Slide 1. The hook and decision

Speaker 1 · from proposal Section 1

Headline pattern: We recommend [the change or the first test] for [the clinic or service]

On the slide: team name and members; the clinic’s challenge question; your three-sentence recommendation.

Speaker notes: introduce the team briefly, read the question, and state the recommendation now rather than at the end.

Practice case headline: “We recommend a one-week timing check of an assisted referral workflow”

Slide 2. Clinic context and evidence

Speaker 1 · from proposal Sections 1–2

Headline pattern: [Who or what] [takes, costs, or affects] [the baseline number and unit]

On the slide: who is affected at the clinic; the key inputs, each labeled observed, estimated, or assumed; a source line at the bottom.

Speaker notes: explain why the problem matters locally, then hand over to Speaker 2.

Practice case headline: “Twenty referral packets a week take about 200 staff minutes”

Slide 3. The proposed solution

Speaker 2 · from proposal Section 2

Headline pattern: [Who] [does what differently]

On the slide: a simple workflow diagram with current practice next to the proposed change; who does the work and who oversees it.

Speaker notes: walk through one case from start to finish under the new workflow.

Practice case headline: “Staff review every assisted packet before it is sent”

Slide 4. Why it is solid

Speaker 2 · from proposal Section 3

Headline pattern: Under [the assumptions], [the change] uses [result] instead of [baseline]

On the slide: the main comparison table or chart from your supporting artifact, with no more than four measures, each labeled measured or modeled.

Speaker notes: say what the comparison shows and what it does not.

Practice case headline: “Under the teaching assumptions, assisted handling uses 140 instead of 200 minutes a week”

Slide 5. Impact on the rural clinic

Speaker 2 · from proposal Section 4

Headline pattern: [Capacity effect], [cost effect], and [access effect]

On the slide: three columns: staff capacity, cost and resources (setup and recurring), and community access.

Speaker notes: keep the three separate; say which effects are tested and which are not. Then hand over to Speaker 3.

Practice case headline: “One staff hour freed, $20 a week in new cash cost, and an access benefit not yet tested”

Slide 6. Limitations and next step

Speaker 3 · from proposal Section 5

Headline pattern: If [the key assumption] passes [a stated value], [the recommendation changes]

On the slide: the key assumption and its breaking point; how AI outputs were checked; the next validation step with who, what, how long, and the result that would change the decision.

Speaker notes: end on the next step, then invite questions and pass each one to its lead speaker.

Practice case headline: “Above about 8 minutes per packet, the freed time no longer covers the subscription”

Question card

Judges will ask these four questions. Prepare a 30-second answer for each and give each one a lead speaker.

QuestionLead speakerOur 30-second answer
What is your weakest assumption?[ ][The assumption, its breaking point, and what you would test]
How good is your data?[ ][Which inputs are observed, estimated, or assumed, and why]
How did you verify AI?[ ][The task, the check, the result, and the limitation]
What would change your recommendation?[ ][The condition and the value at which it applies]

Before you present

  • Slide 1 states the recommendation, and slide 6 states the next step.
  • Every headline is a sentence that states a point.
  • Every number matches the proposal and the supporting artifact.
  • Each slide has one main visual and no more than three short bullets.
  • The team has rehearsed with a timer and finishes in under eight minutes.
  • Each of the four questions has a lead speaker and a prepared answer.
