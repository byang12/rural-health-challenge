Tutorial 6 · Topic 1 of 5

The 4–5 page proposal blueprint

Reading time: about 6 minutes

In this topic

Organize your solution, evidence, and impact on the rural clinic into five proposal sections.

You will leave with a section-by-section outline of your 4–5 page proposal.

Who reads your proposal

Your proposal has two kinds of readers. A clinic manager or community partner reads it to decide on a next step. Judges read it to see whether your reasoning holds up. Neither has time to search for your point. Both need to find the recommendation, the evidence behind it, and its limits quickly.

You are not starting from a blank page. By Tutorial 6 your team has a question (Tutorial 1), checked inputs (Tutorial 2), a baseline comparison (Tutorial 3), a cost and staffing picture (Tutorial 4), and an AI check (Tutorial 5). The proposal puts that work in an order a busy reader can follow.

The five sections at a glance

SectionLengthWhat it answersBuilt from
1. Executive summary and problem½–1 pageWhat do you recommend, and what problem does it address?Tutorial 1 question; Tutorial 3 baseline
2. Proposed solution and evidenceAbout 1 pageHow would the change work, and which inputs support it?Tutorial 4 description of the change; Tutorial 1–2 inputs
3. Why the solution is solidAbout 1 pageHow does the change compare with current practice?Tutorial 3 comparison; your supporting artifact
4. Impact on the clinicAbout 1 pageWhat changes for staff time, spending, and patients?Tutorial 4 cost table; Tutorial 3 types of value
5. Limitations, AI check, and next step½–1 pageWhat could change the answer, and what should the clinic test first?Tutorial 4 sensitivity test; Tutorial 5 AI check; your Team Log

Together that is about 4½ pages, which keeps you within the 4–5 page limit. References do not count toward the limit. The proposal template already has these headings in place.

Section by section

1. Executive summary and problem statement (½–1 page)

Open with your recommendation in three sentences: the proposed change, the strongest evidence for it, and the next validation step. Then describe the problem: which clinic or service is affected, who feels it, and the constraints the clinic works under, such as staff, hours, budget, or distance.

Practice case: a three-sentence recommendation

“We propose an assisted workflow for preparing the clinic’s referral packets, with staff reviewing every packet before it is sent. Under the teaching assumptions, it would use about 140 instead of 200 staff minutes per week, freeing about one hour, while adding a $20 weekly subscription. As a first step, we recommend timing one typical week of packets (about 20) in the assisted workflow to check whether handling stays fast enough to justify that cost.”

2. Proposed solution and supporting evidence (about 1 page)

Explain how the change would work on an ordinary day: who does the work, what changes in their routine, what tool or resource is needed, and who oversees it. A short before-and-after description or a simple diagram is enough.

Then list the 4–6 inputs your recommendation depends on. Copy them from your Tutorial 1 information plan and Tutorial 2 data notes rather than from memory. For each input, give the value, the unit, the source, and whether it is observed, estimated, or assumed.

Every value in the practice case is a fictional teaching assumption. In your project, some inputs may be observed or estimated by a partner. Say which.

3. Why the solution is solid: the baseline comparison (about 1 page)

Show current practice (the baseline) next to the proposed change. Use one table or chart from your supporting artifact (TeamName_Analysis) and compare no more than four measures. Label a result as measured if it comes from a documented test, or modeled if it is calculated from inputs.

Baseline compared with the proposed change in the practice case
MeasureCurrent practiceProposed change
Staff time per week200 minutes140 minutes (modeled)
Staff time freed per weekNone60 minutes (modeled)
Recurring cash costNone$20 per week
One-time setupNone3 staff hours, valued at $90

After the table, explain in two or three sentences what the comparison shows and what it does not. In the practice case, the 60 minutes are a modeled result from assumed inputs, not a measurement at a clinic.

4. Impact on the rural hospital or clinic (about 1 page)

Describe the impact in three separate parts. A gain in one does not prove a gain in another, so do not combine them into a single “savings” figure.

  • Staff capacity. How much staff time is freed, and what useful work could fill it? Practice case: about 60 minutes per week, which could support patient follow-up calls if the clinic chooses to use it that way.
  • Financial and resource effects. Separate one-time setup from recurring cash costs. Practice case: about 3 staff hours of setup, valued at $90, and a $20 weekly subscription. Payroll does not change.
  • Community and patient access. How might local residents get more dependable service? Say what is plausible and what has not been tested. Practice case: faster packet preparation could help referrals move sooner, but nothing in the case shows that it does.

5. Limitations, AI check, and next validation step (½–1 page)

Name the assumption that could change your recommendation and the point at which it would. Explain how AI supported your work and how you checked its important outputs. Then propose one small test the clinic could run first: who does what, for how long, what is measured, and which result would change the decision.

Practice case: limitations and next step

The weakest assumption is the 7-minute assisted handling time. At about 8 minutes per packet, the value of the freed time (about $20 per week) only equals the subscription. At 10 minutes, the time saving disappears. This comparison leaves out the one-time setup, and freed staff time is not cash: the clinic must still find $20 a week in its budget. The team checked its AI by running it on the practice referral packets, comparing every answer item by item with the answer key, and sorting each error into a miss or a false alarm (Tutorial 5). Next step: time one typical week of packets in the assisted workflow, including review, and continue only if handling stays below 8 minutes per packet.

Your Team Log (Topic 5) already holds most of this section: the numbers that changed, the decisions you made, and each AI check.

Practice activity

Try it: outline your proposal

Open the proposal template. Under each of the five headings, write two or three bullet points and note where each one comes from: a tutorial activity, your spreadsheet, or your Team Log. A section with no source yet is the gap to close before you start drafting.

Check your outline

  • Does the first paragraph state the recommendation, the strongest evidence, and the next step?
  • Does every input in Section 2 have a value, unit, source, and type?
  • Does the comparison in Section 3 use no more than four measures, taken from your supporting artifact?
  • Are staff capacity, cash costs, and community access described separately in Section 4?
  • Does Section 5 name the assumption that could change the recommendation, and a test small enough for the clinic to run?
