Submit and Judging

Submission and Judging

Three outputs for one focused project, and the criteria judges will use to assess them.

Submit one focused project

Your submission should investigate one sustainability problem, compare current practice with one proposed improvement, and explain what the available evidence supports.

Final submissions and presentations will take place in January 2027. Exact dates, submission times, and presentation arrangements will be announced on the Timeline page.

Open the final submission form Available soon

Required outputs

Improvement proposal

4–5 pages, excluding references. Describe the problem, evidence, change, comparison, relevant costs and staffing, community value, limitations, and next step.

Supporting analysis or demonstration

One focused artifact: a spreadsheet, map, simple simulation, or small workflow demonstration. Include the inputs, sources, assumptions, and result needed to understand it.

Team presentation

5–7 slides for an eight-minute presentation, followed by questions.

The analysis is the evidence behind the same proposal and presentation. A full clinic feasibility study, a comprehensive business plan, custom software, and extensive new data collection are optional extensions.

File preparation

Submit a readable proposal file and presentation file, together with your supporting artifact or a working link to it. Keep an editable copy of a spreadsheet or model so reviewers can inspect the calculation. A short recording or screenshot can support a demonstration if access to the live tool is limited.

Use clear filenames, such as TeamName_Proposal, TeamName_Analysis, and TeamName_Presentation. Confirm that reviewers can open every link. The final submission instructions will state the accepted formats and file-size limits.

What judges will assess

CriterionWhat a strong submission shows
Problem and community relevanceA focused question, a clear affected population or service, and a convincing reason it matters locally.
Evidence and traceabilityAppropriate sources, consistent definitions, visible assumptions, and checks of important inputs.
Comparison and reasoningA fair baseline-versus-change comparison and an explanation of what could change the recommendation.
Feasibility and valueRealistic staff and resource needs, relevant costs, and an honest account of potential community benefits.
Judgment about AIA defined role for AI, verification of important outputs, and an explanation of its value and limits.
Communication and collaborationA clear recommendation, an understandable supporting artifact, and coordinated contributions across the team.

The final scoring procedure will be shared before evaluation. The same published requirements and criteria apply to all teams. Extra volume and complex software do not receive an automatic advantage.

Claims and limitations

Label a measured task result as measured, a model result as modeled, and an assumption as assumed. Explain separately any financial return, potential staff capacity, and change in community access. A small, well-supported finding can make a useful recommendation.

Before you submit

  • Check that the proposal answers your original question.
  • Confirm that the supporting artifact uses the same inputs and definitions as the proposal.
  • Verify links, sources, and any AI-supported facts or calculations.
  • Identify the most important uncertainty and a feasible next validation step.
  • Check the final posted deadline and submit using the program’s instructions.

The winning team will be announced in January 2027. Any follow-on work will be discussed separately with the relevant team and partners.
